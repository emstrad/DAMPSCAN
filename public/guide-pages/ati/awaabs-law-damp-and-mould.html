Awaab's Law: timescales, damp and mould, and independent surveys

A large share of London homes are rented, many of them from councils and housing associations, and damp and mould complaints are among the most common disputes. Awaab's Law has changed what social landlords in England must do when damp and mould are reported. This page summarises the position in general terms. It is not legal advice, and the requirements are being extended in stages, so check current government guidance for the detail.

Where the law came from

Awaab Ishak died in December 2020, aged two, after prolonged exposure to mould in his family's rented home in Rochdale. The coroner's findings led to the Social Housing (Regulation) Act 2023, which gave the government power to set legal timescales for social landlords to deal with hazards. The first requirements took effect in England in October 2025.

What social landlords must do

Councils and housing associations must investigate reported damp and mould hazards within a fixed period, give the tenant a written summary of what they found, and carry out the necessary work within set timescales. Where a home cannot be made safe in time, the landlord must offer suitable alternative accommodation while the work is done.

The Awaab's Law timescales

The regulations in force since October 2025 set out, in outline, the following. Check the current government guidance for the exact wording, because the definitions of an emergency and a significant hazard, and when the clock starts, matter in any real case.

  • Emergency hazards: investigated and made safe within 24 hours
  • Significant damp and mould hazards: investigated within 10 working days of the landlord becoming aware
  • A written summary of the findings to the tenant within 3 working days of the investigation
  • Significant hazards made safe within 5 working days of the investigation
  • Further work to stop the problem coming back started within a reasonable time, with a published plan
  • Alternative accommodation offered where the home cannot be made safe in time

What is coming next

The first stage covers damp and mould and all emergency hazards. The government has said the same approach will be extended to other hazards under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System in later stages, such as excess cold and heat, falls, fire and electrical hazards. Social landlords in London are already reorganising repairs around these deadlines, which is why independent investigation is in more demand than it was.

Private landlords and the Renters' Rights Act

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 gives the government power to apply Awaab's Law to private rented homes, with the timescales and start date set by later regulations. Until then, private landlords in England already have duties: damp and mould growth is assessed as a hazard under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System, which councils enforce, and homes must be fit for human habitation, which tenants can enforce themselves.

For a London landlord with a mould complaint, the practical point is the same either way: find the cause quickly, write down what you found, and fix that. A record made at the time, with readings, is worth far more than a description written months later in a dispute.

Why the cause is the whole question

Most damp and mould disputes come down to cause. Condensation, a leak from the flat above, rain through a parapet, a cold concrete lintel and a failed extract fan all produce mould, and each needs different action from a different person. A landlord who cleans and repaints without investigating, or who blames the tenant without measuring, is unlikely to resolve the hazard.

An independent survey records the cause with readings and photographs: moisture at depth, humidity, surface temperatures and extraction performance, plus the condition of the building outside the flat. ATi carries out no remedial work and has no interest in the outcome, which is why tenants, housing officers, managing agents and solicitors all use our reports.

What an independent damp survey records

An investigation under Awaab's Law has to establish what the hazard is and why it is there. A survey for a social landlord, managing agent or tenant records the evidence that answers both questions, in a written report that can sit behind the landlord's own summary to the tenant.

  • Moisture readings at the surface and at depth, mapped across the affected walls
  • Air temperature, humidity and dew point, room by room
  • Surface temperatures at cold spots, corners, lintels and slab edges, the evidence for condensation and cold bridging
  • Whether kitchen and bathroom extraction works and actually vents outside
  • The building outside the flat: gutters, downpipes, parapets, balconies, render and the flat above
  • Photographs of every finding, and the work needed in order of priority

Working to the deadlines

Where a landlord or managing agent needs an investigation inside the window, we usually visit within a few days and the written report follows the next working day. We carry out no remedial work, so the report specifies what is needed in terms any contractor can price, and says plainly when the cause is not what it first appeared to be.

Practical steps

Whatever the tenure, the same habits make damp and mould easier to resolve and harder to dispute.

  • Tenants: report damp and mould in writing, keep dated photos, and keep copies of replies
  • Landlords: respond promptly in writing, investigate the cause, and record what you found and did
  • Both: make sure kitchen and bathroom extraction works and vents to outside
  • Both: deal with leaks, rainwater defects and cold spots, not just the visible mould

Questions

Does Awaab's Law apply to my private rented flat?

It currently applies to social housing. Similar requirements can be extended to private renting through further regulations, so check current guidance. Private landlords already have duties under housing health and safety rules and the fitness for habitation requirement.

What counts as a damp and mould hazard?

In general terms, damp and mould that poses a risk to the health or safety of the people living there. Official guidance sets out how landlords should assess it.

Can a tenant commission an independent survey?

Yes. Tenants often do, to get an independent account of the cause to put to their landlord or adviser.

Can a housing association use your reports?

Yes. Landlords and managing agents commission our surveys where they want an independent view, particularly in disputed cases.

Does the law require damp proofing?

No. It requires hazards to be investigated and dealt with. The right work depends on the cause and is often ventilation, heating, leak repair or rainwater work.

How long does a landlord have to investigate damp and mould?

Under the social housing regulations in force since October 2025, an emergency hazard must be investigated and made safe within 24 hours, and a significant damp and mould hazard investigated within 10 working days. Check the current guidance for the exact rules in your case.

Does Awaab's Law apply to private landlords yet?

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 allows it to be applied to private renting, with the start date and timescales set by later regulations. Private landlords already have duties under the housing health and safety rules and the fitness for habitation requirement.

Is this legal advice?

No. For a specific case, speak to a solicitor, a housing adviser or your council's housing team.
