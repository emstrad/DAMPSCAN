Air conditioning running but not cooling

The unit is on, the fan is blowing, and the room is no cooler. It is the commonest call we get in the first hot week of the year, and in a fair share of cases the cause is something simple. In the rest it is a fault that needs an engineer with gauges, and guessing at it costs more than finding it.

Signs to look for

  • Air from the indoor unit that feels barely cooler than the room
  • The outdoor unit not running, or starting and stopping every few minutes
  • Ice or frost on the pipes or on the indoor coil
  • A flashing light or an error code on the indoor unit
  • One room in a multi-split cooling and another not
  • Cooling that fades on the hottest afternoons

What causes it

Blocked filters are the first thing to check. A clogged filter starves the coil of air, cooling falls away and the coil can ice over. Remote settings come next: a unit left in fan, dry or heat mode, or set to a temperature above the room, will not cool.

Low refrigerant means there is a leak somewhere, because a sealed system does not use gas. Topping it up without finding the leak only buys a few weeks. Faulty capacitors, fan motors and sensors also stop the outdoor unit running properly, and a dirty outdoor coil or one boxed in by plants or a cover cannot reject heat.

Some units are simply too small for the room, which only shows on the hottest days. That is a sizing problem from the original installation rather than a fault.

What we do

Before booking, check the filters, the mode and the set temperature, and that nothing is blocking the outdoor unit. If that does not sort it, we attend with the gauges and test equipment to diagnose the fault properly: pressures, temperatures, electrical components and error history.

If refrigerant is low we find the leak before regassing, and we tell you what the repair involves. If the system is undersized, we say so plainly rather than selling repairs that will not change the outcome.

What it usually costs

A diagnostic visit is quoted before we come. What the repair then costs depends on the fault: a capacitor or sensor is a modest part, while a leak repair and regas involves more time and refrigerant. We tell you the cost of the repair before doing it, in writing, and you decide.

Questions

Can I just have it regassed?

You can, but if the gas has gone it has leaked out, and it will leak out again. We find and fix the leak first, which is also what the refrigerant regulations expect.

Why is there ice on my air con pipes?

Usually restricted airflow from dirty filters, or low refrigerant. Switch the unit off to let it thaw, clean the filters, and call us if it comes back.

Is it worth repairing an old unit?

Often, yes. If it uses an older refrigerant or the compressor has failed, replacement may make more sense, and we will give you both figures.
