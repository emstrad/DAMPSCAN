When your air conditioning smells

An air conditioner draws the air of the room through a cold, wet coil all summer, which is a perfect place for mould and bacteria to grow if it is not cleaned. A musty smell when the unit starts is therefore common and fixable. Other smells, burning in particular, mean something different and need a different response.

Signs to look for

  • A musty, damp or "old socks" smell when the unit first starts
  • A sour or stale smell that lingers in the room
  • Black spots visible on the louvres or the fan inside the unit
  • A burning or electrical smell
  • A sweet or chemical smell near the unit or the pipework

What causes it

Musty smells come from mould and biofilm on the indoor coil, the drip tray and the barrel shaped fan. Units that are rarely serviced, or run on dry mode in humid weather and then switched off wet, are the most affected.

A burning smell can be dust burning off a heating coil on the first cold morning, which passes quickly, or an electrical fault, which does not. A sweet or chemical smell can be a refrigerant leak, and a smell of drains usually means a dry trap or a badly connected condensate pipe drawing air back from the waste.

What we do

For musty smells we deep clean the indoor unit: coil, tray, drain and fan, using cleaners made for the job, and we treat the drain so it stays clear. Where the fan is heavily contaminated we remove it to clean it properly rather than spraying it in place.

For burning or chemical smells, switch the unit off at the isolator and call us. We test the electrics and the refrigerant circuit, find the cause and quote the repair. Drain smells are fixed by correcting the condensate connection.

What it usually costs

A deep clean of an indoor unit costs more than a routine service, because the unit is partly dismantled, and less than most repairs. Electrical or refrigerant faults are quoted after diagnosis. Annual servicing keeps most smells from developing in the first place.

Questions

Is the musty smell harmful?

It is mould and bacteria, which people with asthma or allergies can react to. It is worth cleaning rather than masking with a spray.

How can I stop it coming back?

Keep the filters clean, have the unit serviced every year, and let it run on fan for a while before switching off so the coil dries.

My unit smells of burning. What should I do?

Switch it off at the isolator and do not use it until it has been checked. A brief smell on the first heating day is usually dust, but a persistent one is not.
