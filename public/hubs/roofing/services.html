What we do, and when we tell you not to

Roofing is sold on fear more than any other trade. A roof is the one part of a house nobody can see, the quote arrives after somebody has been up a ladder alone, and the person who found the fault is the person selling the cure. That arrangement pays for a lot of roofs that did not need replacing.

Each page below covers one job on its own terms: what it actually involves, what it is most often confused with, and what moves the figure up or down. Several of them spend their length explaining how to tell when you do not need the work at all.

We quote every job rather than publishing a rate card, because a roof is a building and not a product. What we will do is tell you what is included, what is not, and what would be discussed if we open up and find something neither of us could see.

Every service, in detail

  • Commercial roofing Industrial units, warehouses, retail parades, schools and managed blocks: single ply, built-up felt, liquid coatings and sheet repairs, programmed around occupancy.
  • Flat roofing GRP, EPDM and torch-on felt compared honestly, why most failed flat roofs fail for the same two reasons, and what a proper job corrects.
  • Guttering, fascias and soffits Rainwater goods are unglamorous and cause an enormous amount of damage when they fail. Clearing, realigning, repair and full renewal.
  • Leadwork and chimneys Chimney stacks, parapets, valleys and abutments: where most roofs leak, and why a mortar fillet is doing a job only lead can do.
  • Full re-roofs When a roof is worth replacing rather than patching, what a full strip and re-cover involves, and what moves the price up or down.
  • Roof maintenance Annual and six-monthly roof checks with a photo report each time, so the history of the roof exists somewhere other than memory.
  • Roof repairs Slipped tiles, failed flashings, leaks nobody has traced. What a proportionate repair involves, and how the cause is found rather than the wet patch.
  • Storm damage Make-safe first, then a dated photographic report and a scoped quotation in the format loss adjusters accept.
