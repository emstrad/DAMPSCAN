Read this before you pay for treatment

Most of the money wasted on damp and timber in this region is spent before anyone has established what is wrong. These guides are written for the point before that, when you are holding a quote or a valuation comment and want to know what it should mean.

We carry out no remedial work, which is why these pages can say plainly when it is not needed. A firm that treats a historic woodworm infestation or sprays a whole house for one soft joist end has earned an invoice and nothing else, and we would rather write the report that says so.

Each guide gives the figures quotes tend to fall in, the questions that separate a fair scope from a padded one, and the cases where the right answer is a repair, a repair to the water, or nothing at all.

  • How much does a damp survey cost in Kent? What decides the cost of a damp survey in Kent, why some are free and others are not, and what you should get for the fee. Plain answers before you book.
  • Do I need a damp survey when buying a house? When a buyer in Kent needs a separate damp survey, what a homebuyer report can and cannot tell you about damp, and how to use the findings in negotiation.
  • Landlord damp and mould duties, and Awaab's Law A plain guide for Kent landlords and agents to damp and mould duties: Awaab's Law in social housing, what applies to private lets, and how a survey helps.
  • Rising damp or condensation? How to tell How to tell rising damp from condensation and penetrating damp in a Kent home: where it appears, when it is worst, and the checks you can do yourself first.
  • What woodworm and rot treatment costs What woodworm, wet rot and dry rot treatment costs in Kent and the South East, what drives the bill, and the common cases where no treatment is needed at all.
