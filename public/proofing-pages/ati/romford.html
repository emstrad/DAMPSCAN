The first almost always has a working damp proof course, and the second needs its walls to dry rather than be sealed. Why the survey comes before damp proofing in Romford

In Romford the question is rarely whether a damp proof course has failed. It is who is responsible for the fault that is wetting the wall, and what will actually stop it. That might be a freeholder, a neighbour or a managing agent rather than you. An independent report answers both, and we carry out no remedial work, so the answer is not shaped by a sale.

The quote we see most often in Romford is injection along the front of a 1930s semi where the drive was relaid higher than the course, with a metre of dense replastering inside. The ground outside keeps bridging the course, the new plaster holds the moisture at the surface, and the stain comes back above it. The other is damp proofing proposed for black mould in a bedroom, which is condensation and is untouched by any course.

If you are a landlord in Romford, remember that the hazard is the damp and mould, not the absence of a damp proof course. Removing the cause is what deals with it, and that is often ventilation, heating, rainwater or a leak. If you are buying, a report before exchange tells you whether a damp proofing recommendation in the homebuyer survey is justified, and what the honest scope of work is.

What the different damp proofing treatments are for

Injection is meant for rising damp in a wall with no working course. Tanking and cavity drain membranes are for below ground walls, which in Romford usually means a lower ground floor or a cellar. Salt resistant replastering deals with contamination left by long term damp. Ventilation and heating deal with condensation. Each is valid for its own fault and useless for the others.

London's solid walled terraces were built to breathe. Sealing one with cement render or tanking above ground tends to push moisture somewhere else in the building, frequently into the flat next door or the floor above.

Romford walls, and what they actually need

Romford's cavity walled semis stay dry when the drive and paths sit two courses below the damp proof course, the cavity base is clear and the airbricks under the floor are open. Its Victorian terraces are solid brick that needs lime or breathable plaster, sound pointing, and render that lets the wall dry, and the Gidea Park houses need their roughcast and tile hanging kept in order.

  • Lowering drives and patios, or adding a drained channel, where they bridge the damp proof course
  • Reopening blocked airbricks and clearing debris from the base of cavities
  • Replacing dense cement plaster on solid walls with lime or a breathable plaster
  • Better extraction and ventilation where the problem is condensation and mould

Why the drive matters more than the wall

A damp proof course only works while the ground outside stays below it. On thousands of Romford semis, drives have been relaid, block paved or extended over the years, each time a little higher, until the paving sits level with the course or above it. Rain then splashes and soaks straight past it, and the wall above shows a tide mark that looks exactly like rising damp.

The repair is outside: lower the paving at the wall, or cut a drained gravel channel along it, and let the wall dry. It costs a fraction of injection and replastering, and it deals with the cause. Much of Romford sits on London Clay, which drains slowly, so getting surface water away from the walls matters more here than on lighter ground.

What proper damp proofing involves in a London building

  • A diagnosis in writing, with readings, before any specification
  • External and communal faults identified and assigned: parapets, gutters, lightwells, shared drains
  • A new damp proof course only on a Romford wall that genuinely lacks a working one at low level
  • Breathable or salt resistant replastering chosen for the wall, not a default system
  • Consent considered where the building is listed or in a conservation area
  • A record of what was done and why, which a future buyer's surveyor will ask for

Avoiding an unnecessary damp proofing bill

  • Treat a free survey as a sales visit and do not sign on the day
  • Ask how condensation, rainwater and ground level were excluded
  • Ask whether any of the cause sits in the common parts and who should pay
  • Be cautious of tanking an above ground wall in a Romford conversion
  • Ask for the readings, their locations and the height of plaster removal in writing
  • If a purchase depends on it, get the report before exchange, not after

When a damp proof course is the right answer

We are not opposed to damp proofing, only to it being sold as the answer before the question has been asked. Where the readings, the pattern and the building all point to a missing or failed course in a Romford property, the report recommends one and says how it should be done.

Set the fixed survey fee against the cost of injection, replastering and redecoration in a Romford property, and against the cost of repeating it when the cause was elsewhere. The comparison usually makes the decision.

The report gives you the cause, the responsibility and a specification. In Romford that often means a letter to a freeholder or managing agent with the evidence attached, before any work inside the flat is considered.

Damp proofing questions in Romford

We have been quoted for a new damp proof course on our Romford semi. Do we need one?

Rarely. Interwar and post-war houses usually have a working course. Check the paving outside first: if it is within about two courses of the course, lowering it or adding a drained channel is the usual answer. The survey confirms it with readings.

Our Victorian terrace near the market has damp walls. Should we inject?

Solid brick walls of that age stay dry when they can breathe. Cement render, cement pointing and gypsum or sand and cement plaster trap moisture in them. The survey identifies what is holding the water before any treatment is considered.

There is black mould in our bedroom. Is that a damp proofing job?

No. Mould is condensation, and a damp proof course cannot affect it. Cold corners, weak extract fans and closed trickle vents are the usual causes, and the report says which matter in your home.
