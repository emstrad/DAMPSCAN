Why the survey comes before damp proofing in Sittingbourne

A free damp survey in Sittingbourne is usually a sales visit with a meter. The meter reads high on a wall that is wet for any reason, and on salts in old plaster even when the wall is dry, so a high reading proves very little. An independent survey finds the cause, and only the cause tells you whether damp proofing is the remedy at all.

The Sittingbourne quote we see most often is chemical injection on a Victorian terrace with a metre of sand and cement replastering, often with a waterproof additive. On stock brick, that combination seals the inside face of a wall that may already have been sealed outside by cement pointing. The wall stays wet, salts collect at the edge of the new plaster and the damp line moves up.

Injection, tanking, membranes: what they are for

Injection is for rising damp: groundwater drawn up a wall that has no working course. Tanking and cavity drain membranes are for walls below ground that hold back soil and water, such as cellars and basements. Salt resistant replastering is for walls where years of damp have left salts in the old plaster. Ventilation is for condensation. A quote that applies one of them to the wrong problem will fail however well it is installed.

In Sittingbourne the commonest mismatch we see is injection and a cement based replaster on a wall whose real trouble is outside: open joints, raised ground or a leaking downpipe. Fix those and the wall often dries without anything being injected at all.

Sittingbourne walls, and what they actually need

Yellow stock brick in lime needs to breathe. That means lime or breathable plaster inside, lime pointing outside, ground and paths kept below the course, and downpipes discharging into drains rather than at the wall. The newer homes need their ventilation working and their junctions detailed properly.

Sittingbourne lies on brickearth and London Clay sloping gently down to the Swale, with Milton Creek cutting into the town from the north. The creek side and the marsh edges are low and wet, with a high water table and tidal influence. Clay drains slowly, so gardens stay wet in winter and water lingers at the base of walls. To the south, the land rises onto the chalk of the Downs at Borden and Tunstall, where drainage is much better.

Brickearth, brick and the right mortar

Sittingbourne's yellow stocks were made from local brickearth with chalk and ash mixed in, fired in clamps, and laid in lime. They are relatively soft and porous by modern standards, which is fine as long as the mortar is softer still and the wall can dry. A hard cement repoint reverses that relationship, and moisture leaves through the face of the brick, which then spalls in frost.

The repair is to rake out the cement carefully, so as not to damage the arrises of the brick, and repoint in a lime mortar. Inside, sand and cement or gypsum plaster should be replaced with lime or a breathable lightweight plaster. Combined with dealing with raised ground and leaking downpipes, that resolves most low level damp in the town's terraces without any injected course.

Damp proofing done properly, in the right order

  • Find the cause: measure, look outside, rule the alternatives in or out
  • Fix the cheap external faults first and give the wall time to respond
  • Only then, if a yellow stock brick or brick wall is still wet at low level with no working course, consider a new damp proof course
  • Replaster with materials that suit the wall: lime on old solid walls, never cement on soft brick or stone
  • Ventilate and heat so the wall can dry, and check it again after a season
  • Keep the paperwork: diagnosis, specification, guarantee and the readings after

Questions to put to anyone quoting

  • Why is this wall wet, and what is the evidence?
  • Have you checked the ground level, gutters and pointing outside, and what did you find?
  • Would the damp stop if those were fixed, and how do you know it would not?
  • Why this height of replastering, and why this material?
  • What exactly does the guarantee cover if the damp comes back in this Sittingbourne house?
  • Can I have the survey in writing before I decide?

Damp proofing questions in Sittingbourne

Is chemical injection right for stock brick?

Occasionally, where there is no working course and nothing external explains the damp. In most Sittingbourne terraces we survey, the course still works and the damp has another cause.

Why does sand and cement plaster make things worse?

It is dense and impermeable. On a solid wall it stops moisture leaving through the inner face, so it moves higher and salts collect at the plaster line. Lime and breathable plasters let the wall dry.

How much damp proofing is normal for a Sittingbourne terrace?

There is no normal amount. The right amount is what the cause requires, which may be none. The report specifies exactly what, if anything, is needed.

We have a quote and a deadline to exchange. Can you help quickly?

Yes. We reply the same day and can often visit within days. The report follows within 24 hours of the visit.

Does the survey cover woodworm too?

Yes. Accessible timbers are checked for decay and beetle activity, with moisture readings to show whether any activity is current.

What if a damp proofing firm disagrees with your report?

Ask them to explain their evidence against the readings and photographs in the report. A diagnosis based on measurements is much easier to test than one based on a single meter reading.

Do you carry out damp proofing in Sittingbourne?

No. We survey and report only, and we take no commission from contractors. That is what lets the report say a wall does not need damp proofing when it does not. Where it does, the specification is written for you to get like for like quotes.
