Why the survey comes before damp proofing in Snodland

Damp proofing is the answer to one specific fault, a missing or failed damp proof course, and that fault is far rarer in Snodland than the quotes suggest. Before anyone drills a wall, somebody with nothing to sell should establish why it is wet. That is the whole of what we do. We carry out no treatment, so the survey is not a route to a sale.

On the terraces, the quote is usually injection along the rear wall and into the party wall, with replastering. The rear wall is wet because of the yard or a downpipe, and the party wall rarely has external ground against it. On the new estates, firms sometimes quote damp proofing for mould, which is condensation and a ventilation matter.

A short guide to damp proofing methods

Chemical injection puts a water repellent barrier into the base of a wall. Electro osmotic and other proprietary systems make similar claims by different means. Membranes and tanking hold or drain water on below ground walls. Breathable or salt resistant plasters deal with what years of damp leave behind. They are tools, and the skill is in knowing which, if any, the wall needs.

Old Snodland walls built in Victorian brick or soft brick were never meant to be sealed. They take a little water and lose it again, and many damp problems in them start the day somebody stopped them losing it. The right damp proofing for such a wall is often the work that lets it breathe again.

Snodland walls, and what they actually need

Snodland's terraces have solid brick walls and, in many cases, original slate courses that work when uncovered. They need the ground and the yard dealt with, gutters and downpipes discharging into drains, and breathable plaster inside. The newer homes have cavity walls with modern membranes and need their ventilation working.

Snodland sits between the river and the chalk of the North Downs. The low ground near the Medway is alluvium and gravels with a high water table, while the land rises to chalk towards Holborough and Paddlesworth, where quarrying for cement left deep pits now flooded as lakes. Streets close to the river are low lying and the river is tidal up to Allington, so groundwater responds to both rain and tide.

  • Lowering or re laying rear yards so they fall away from the house
  • Connecting downpipes to drains rather than discharging at the base of the wall
  • Breathable or lime plaster on solid walls, where old plaster is salt contaminated
  • Ventilation repairs and commissioning on newer homes with mould

Yards, outbuildings and former outside toilets

Many Snodland terraces have had their rear yards concreted, sometimes more than once, and their outside toilets and coal stores turned into utility rooms or extended into kitchens. Each of those changes can bridge the base of the original wall. A concrete yard laid against the back wall and falling towards it is one of the most reliable ways to wet a terrace, and the damp shows in the kitchen or back room.

Where an outbuilding has been joined to the house, the junction often has no cavity tray or damp proof course detail, and the old outside wall, now inside, gets wet from the roof and the floor of the newer part. The survey traces these junctions, and the repair is to the junction rather than to the whole house.

What proper damp proofing should look like

  • A written diagnosis first, with readings and photographs, saying why the wall is wet
  • External causes fixed before or alongside any internal work: ground levels, gutters, pointing, render
  • A damp proof course only where one is missing or has genuinely failed, at the right height for the Snodland house in question
  • Salt contaminated plaster removed and replaced with a suitable breathable or salt resistant finish, not sealed over
  • Drying time allowed before decoration, because a wall that has been wet for years does not dry in a week
  • A guarantee that names the walls treated and the cause addressed, from a firm that will still be there

Questions to put to anyone quoting

  • Why is this wall wet, and what is the evidence?
  • Have you checked the ground level, gutters and pointing outside, and what did you find?
  • Would the damp stop if those were fixed, and how do you know it would not?
  • Why this height of replastering, and why this material?
  • What exactly does the guarantee cover if the damp comes back in this Snodland house?
  • Can I have the survey in writing before I decide?

When a damp proof course is the right answer

We are not against damp proofing. We are against it being the default. When a Snodland wall has no working course, sits on damp ground and stays wet after the outside has been put right, a damp proof course and suitable replastering is the right job, and the report will say that and specify it.

Damp proofing questions in Snodland

Do party walls in terraces need damp proofing?

Rarely. They are internal, sheltered and usually have no ground against them. Damp at the base of a party wall is more often a leak, a solid floor bridging it, or salts in old plaster.

Can damp proofing stop groundwater on the low streets?

Not a standard injected course. Floors affected by groundwater need a construction suited to wet ground, and sometimes drainage. The survey checks what is happening before anything is chosen.

A firm said our new build needs a damp proof course. Is that likely?

Very unlikely. New homes are built with damp proof courses and membranes. If there is damp, it is more likely to be ventilation, construction moisture or a detail fault, which may be the developer's responsibility.

What should a good damp proofing quote include?

The cause, which walls and how high, the plaster specification, any external work to be done first, drying time and what the guarantee covers. Ask for all of these in writing.

Can you look at our quote before we sign?

Yes. Send it with your booking and the report will say which parts are supported by the survey.

Do you carry out repairs?

No. We survey and report only, which is why the report can say when damp proofing is not needed.

Do you carry out damp proofing in Snodland?

No. We survey and report only, and we take no commission from contractors. That is what lets the report say a wall does not need damp proofing when it does not. Where it does, the specification is written for you to get like for like quotes.
