Why the survey comes before damp proofing in Dover

A free damp survey in Dover is usually a sales visit with a meter. The meter reads high on a wall that is wet for any reason, and on salts in old plaster even when the wall is dry, so a high reading proves very little. An independent survey finds the cause, and only the cause tells you whether damp proofing is the remedy at all.

The Dover quotes that concern us most are injection and cement replastering on chalk block and flint walls, and injection on uphill walls that are wet from the slope behind. On chalk block, cement replastering accelerates decay. On retaining walls, injection does nothing for water arriving sideways.

A short guide to damp proofing methods

Chemical injection puts a water repellent barrier into the base of a wall. Electro osmotic and other proprietary systems make similar claims by different means. Membranes and tanking hold or drain water on below ground walls. Breathable or salt resistant plasters deal with what years of damp leave behind. They are tools, and the skill is in knowing which, if any, the wall needs.

Old Dover walls built in flint, chalk block and brick or soft brick were never meant to be sealed. They take a little water and lose it again, and many damp problems in them start the day somebody stopped them losing it. The right damp proofing for such a wall is often the work that lets it breathe again.

Dover walls, and what they actually need

Chalk block and flint need lime render, lime pointing and breathable finishes. Victorian terraces on slopes need drainage on the uphill side and below ground solutions where rooms sit below ground. Seafront walls need sound render and salt tolerant plaster.

Why chalk and flint walls are often harmed by damp proofing

Chalk block is weak when wet and strong when dry. Flint walls rely on lime mortar to stay sound. Both depend on being able to lose water as fast as they take it in. Most damp proofing systems do the opposite: they add a barrier and a dense, waterproof plaster that stop the wall drying on the inside face.

On a chalk block wall that has already been rendered in cement outside, adding a waterproof plaster inside traps the wall between two impermeable skins. Moisture builds up, salts crystallise and the block softens. We have seen walls where the face of the chalk had turned to paste behind the plaster. The repair after that is far more expensive than the treatment was.

The proportionate approach is lime render and lime plaster, ground kept low, rain kept out, and the wall allowed to dry. Where a wall is below ground on the uphill side, drainage and a drained membrane are the answer. The survey establishes which applies before any money is spent.

What proper damp proofing should look like

  • A written diagnosis first, with readings and photographs, saying why the wall is wet
  • External causes fixed before or alongside any internal work: ground levels, gutters, pointing, render
  • A damp proof course only where one is missing or has genuinely failed, at the right height for the Dover house in question
  • Salt contaminated plaster removed and replaced with a suitable breathable or salt resistant finish, not sealed over
  • Drying time allowed before decoration, because a wall that has been wet for years does not dry in a week
  • A guarantee that names the walls treated and the cause addressed, from a firm that will still be there

Red flags in a damp proofing quote

  • A diagnosis of rising damp from a single meter reading
  • No mention of anything outside the house
  • Injection recommended on internal walls, chimney breasts or walls above ground floor
  • A discount that expires today
  • Treatment of woodworm or rot in every timber "as a precaution"
  • A quote for a Dover period house that would be identical for a 1990s semi

When a damp proof course is the right answer

There are walls in Dover that do need a damp proof course. A solid wall built without one, or with a slate or bitumen course that has cracked or been cut through, that is wet at low level with nothing outside to explain it and with the readings falling off with height, is a reasonable candidate. When we find that, the report says so and specifies where and how high, so you can get comparable quotes.

Damp proofing questions in Dover

Can chalk block walls be damp proofed?

They can be kept dry, but not with the usual damp proofing. Chalk block needs lime render outside, lime plaster inside and ground kept low. Injection and waterproof plaster trap moisture in a soft material that weakens when wet, and we have seen walls badly damaged by it.

Does a wall cut into the valley side need a damp proof course?

No. It is retaining ground and is wet from the side. It needs water intercepted uphill of the house and, if a room sits below ground, a below ground system. A horizontal course does nothing for lateral water.

Is salt damp on our seafront wall a reason for damp proofing?

No. Salt from spray draws moisture out of the air into the plaster. Replacing contaminated plaster with a salt tolerant finish and keeping rain out of the wall helps; an injected course does not.

What should a Dover damp proofing quote include?

The cause in writing, which walls and how high, the plaster specification and why it suits the wall, any outside work to be done first, the drying time before decoration, and exactly what the guarantee covers. If those are missing, ask before you sign.

Our Whitfield new build has mould. Is damp proofing needed?

Almost certainly not. Mould in a modern house is condensation, ventilation or a construction detail. The survey measures extraction and humidity, and the report can go to the developer if it is a defect.

Do you carry out work?

No. We survey and report only, which is why the report can tell you when damp proofing is not needed.
