Why the survey comes before damp proofing in Chatham

Most of the walls we are asked to look at in Chatham after a damp proofing quote do not need a new damp proof course. They need the water dealt with at its source: a path lowered, a gutter cleared, red and yellow stock brick repointed in the right mortar, or a fan that actually extracts. Injecting a wall that is wet for another reason costs real money and fixes nothing, and the tide mark returns once the new plaster dries out.

On the hillside terraces, the quote we see most is injection along the rear and party walls and a metre of replastering. The rear wall is wet because it holds back the hill, and the party wall rarely has any ground against it at all. On the estates, mould on a bedroom wall gets quoted for injection or a "condensation package" that includes a damp course, when the problem is cold bridging and extraction.

Injection, tanking, membranes: what they are for

Injection is for rising damp: groundwater drawn up a wall that has no working course. Tanking and cavity drain membranes are for walls below ground that hold back soil and water, such as cellars and basements. Salt resistant replastering is for walls where years of damp have left salts in the old plaster. Ventilation is for condensation. A quote that applies one of them to the wrong problem will fail however well it is installed.

In Chatham the commonest mismatch we see is injection and a cement based replaster on a wall whose real trouble is outside: open joints, raised ground or a leaking downpipe. Fix those and the wall often dries without anything being injected at all.

Chatham walls, and what they actually need

A Chatham terrace is solid stock brick in lime, usually with a slate course that still works at the front. Below ground on the uphill side it needs drainage outside and, where a room sits below the level of the yard, a proper below ground solution rather than injection. A post war house needs its damp proof course left alone and its ventilation and cold spots dealt with.

Chatham is chalk under a thin cover, cut by dry valleys that run down to the Medway. The slopes are steep, so water moves quickly downhill at the uphill side of terraces in heavy rain. The valley floors and the riverside are lower and wetter, and the Walderslade valleys collect surface water from the estates above. Chalk also has a habit of forming solution features and old workings, which occasionally show up as settlement and cracked drains rather than as damp, but a cracked drain under a house can be both.

  • Re laying rear yards to fall away from the house, with a drainage channel at the wall
  • A drained cavity membrane only where a habitable room genuinely sits below external ground
  • Investigating drains on steep plots where damp appears in one sharp patch
  • Ventilation and cold bridge fixes, not damp proof courses, for mould on the estates

What proper damp proofing should look like

  • A written diagnosis first, with readings and photographs, saying why the wall is wet
  • External causes fixed before or alongside any internal work: ground levels, gutters, pointing, render
  • A damp proof course only where one is missing or has genuinely failed, at the right height for the Chatham house in question
  • Salt contaminated plaster removed and replaced with a suitable breathable or salt resistant finish, not sealed over
  • Drying time allowed before decoration, because a wall that has been wet for years does not dry in a week
  • A guarantee that names the walls treated and the cause addressed, from a firm that will still be there

Questions to put to anyone quoting

  • Why is this wall wet, and what is the evidence?
  • Have you checked the ground level, gutters and pointing outside, and what did you find?
  • Would the damp stop if those were fixed, and how do you know it would not?
  • Why this height of replastering, and why this material?
  • What exactly does the guarantee cover if the damp comes back in this Chatham house?
  • Can I have the survey in writing before I decide?

When a damp proof course is the right answer

Genuine rising damp exists, and when the survey finds it the report specifies the remedy plainly. The test is not a meter reading but a pattern: damp highest at the base of the wall, falling with height, present on walls with no external fault, and present in summer as well as winter. In Chatham we find that pattern on a minority of the walls we are asked about.

Set against a damp proofing job on a Chatham house, which typically runs to several times that once plaster removal and making good are included, the survey is small. When it shows that a wall needs a gutter repair rather than an injected course, it pays for itself several times over.

Chatham offers some of the most affordable houses in north Kent, and many of them are Victorian terraces on slopes. A homebuyer report that recommends a damp specialist is common on these. Before exchange, an independent survey establishes whether the damp is lateral water from the hill, a yard falling the wrong way, a leaking drain, or nothing more than salts, and what each would take to fix.

After the survey you have the cause in writing, a list of what needs doing in order, and what does not. If damp proofing is part of it, the report specifies the walls, the height and the finish, so the quotes you collect in Chatham are for the same job. Damp proofing questions in Chatham

Can a damp proof course stop water coming through a retaining wall?

No. A damp proof course is a horizontal barrier against moisture rising from below. A retaining wall is wet from the side, from the soil it holds back. That needs drainage outside, and where the room is below ground, a system designed for below ground walls.

Is tanking or a membrane better for a below ground room in Chatham?

It depends on the water. On chalk, water often arrives with rainfall and drains away, and a drained membrane copes well with that and with old, uneven walls. Tanking suits sound walls with modest pressure. The choice should follow a survey of the water, not a standard price list.

Our neighbour had injection and says it worked. Should we do the same?

Their wall may have had a different cause, or the replastering alone may have hidden the damp for a while. Terraces next door to each other often differ in yards, drains and gutters. A survey of your own house is the only way to know.

Does damp proofing help with mould on the Walderslade estates?

No. Mould is about surface temperature, humidity and air movement. Fixing extraction, insulating a cold lintel or improving heating patterns is what works, and the survey measures which of those matters most.

Will your report stand up if a landlord or developer disagrees?

It is written as evidence: readings, locations, photographs and the reasoning behind each conclusion. That is what makes it useful in a dispute, and because we sell no remedy, it is harder to dismiss as a sales document.

Do you give a price for the work?

We do not price or carry out work. The report gives a specification and, where helpful, an indication of the scale of the job, so you can get comparable quotes and recognise one that is out of line.

Do you carry out damp proofing in Chatham?

No. We survey and report only, and we take no commission from contractors. That is what lets the report say a wall does not need damp proofing when it does not. Where it does, the specification is written for you to get like for like quotes.
