Kingston upon Thames housing, and what it does when it gets wet

Properties in and near the tidal flood plain need looking at differently. Historic flooding leaves salts in masonry that keep a meter reading high for years afterwards, long after the wall itself has dried. A conductivity meter cannot tell salt from water, so a flood affected wall can be repeatedly diagnosed as damp and repeatedly treated for it. Interpretation, not measurement, is what settles that.

The Victorian and Edwardian terraces and villas through Surbiton and Kingston are solid or early cavity brick, largely extended at the rear. The junction between old brickwork and a newer extension roof is the usual entry point, and rear closet wing gutters are the usual blockage. Both produce damp on an internal wall some distance from the defect.

The interwar and post-war housing at New Malden, Chessington and Tolworth is cavity walled with working damp proof courses. The common finding is external ground raised above the course by successive drives and patios, and blocked airbricks under suspended floors, neither of which needs a chemical remedy.

Getting the diagnosis right in a London building

London's housing has been altered more than most: basements dug, lofts converted, houses split into flats, gardens raised, chimneys removed. In Kingston upon Thames that means damp is frequently the result of a change made decades ago rather than of the original building, and the remedy is to undo or correct that change.

We look for those alterations as much as for the damp itself. A floor laid above an old course, a fan ducted into a void, a lightwell filled with decking or a stack capped without ventilation explains more damp than any failed damp proof course.

How we survey a Kingston upon Thames property

  • Externally first: the elevations, the ground or paving against them, and every route water has to the wall
  • For flats, the parts of the building outside the demise that can be seen or reasonably reached
  • Readings that distinguish surface salts from moisture in depth, rather than a single pin meter figure
  • Dew point conditions in the affected rooms, logged and compared with the coldest surfaces
  • Ventilation measured, not assumed from the presence of a fan
  • Previous damp treatments identified, with whether they addressed the cause

Kingston upon Thames questions

The house flooded years ago. Why does it still read damp?

Because flood water leaves salts behind in the masonry, and salts are hygroscopic: they draw moisture from the air and they conduct electricity. A conductivity meter responds to both, so a wall that has dried out completely can still give high readings for years. That is one of the most common reasons flood affected houses are treated repeatedly for damp they no longer have. Distinguishing salt from moisture requires more than a surface meter, and it is worth doing before anyone re plasters again.

We are buying near the river. What should the survey cover?

Flood history and how it was dealt with, first. Then whether the ground floor construction, finishes and services are appropriate for a property that may flood again, and whether previous reinstatement used materials that will survive it. Beyond that it is a normal survey: ground levels, drainage, rainwater goods, roof and extension junctions, and calibrated readings interpreted with the salt question in mind rather than taken at face value.

Our riverside flat in Kingston has damp in the ground floor. Is it the Thames?

On low ground near the river the water table can be high, especially in floors. The survey separates groundwater from rainwater and defects.

Our Surbiton conversion has damp in the top flat. Who fixes it?

Roofs and gutters are commonly the freeholder's responsibility. The report identifies the source.

Do New Malden semis need damp proofing?

Rarely. Their courses usually work; drives and patios are the common cause.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Kingston upon Thames?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
