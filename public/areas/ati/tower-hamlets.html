Tower Hamlets housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The Spitalfields and Whitechapel Georgian houses are among the oldest domestic buildings still in ordinary use in London. Solid brick, lime mortar, timber floors built into the walls, and often a top floor weavers loft with very large windows. They depend on breathing, and a great many have been through unsympathetic refurbishment with gypsum plaster and cement pointing. Decay in timbers built into a wall that can no longer dry is the usual finding, and it is structural.

The post-war blocks and estates, which house a large share of the borough, fail at cold bridges and joints: mould in bands at slab level, at window reveals and in corners, following the structure rather than the room. In a disrepair context that distinction decides the case, so the report has to record the pattern against the building rather than simply confirm that mould exists.

The Docklands towers are the newest problem and in some ways the least well understood. Highly airtight envelopes, mechanical ventilation with heat recovery, floor to ceiling glazing and single aspect layouts. When the ventilation is unbalanced, uncommissioned or switched off because it is noisy, moisture has nowhere to go and mould appears in a two year old flat. That is a building performance defect, not occupant behaviour, and it needs measuring to prove.

Which kind of damp is it?

In a London building the same stain can have four or five different causes, and in Tower Hamlets we regularly find two at once. Water rising from the ground through a wall with no working course. Rain entering through a parapet, a gutter, a cracked render or a window head. Moisture from the air condensing on a cold wall. Water from a pipe, a bath seal or a flat upstairs. Each needs a different repair and, in a shared building, often a different person to pay for it.

Our readings are taken to separate them: at depth as well as at the surface, at heights up the wall, on either side of the damp, and alongside humidity and surface temperatures. The pattern, not a single number, is what the diagnosis rests on.

The inspection, around Whitechapel and across Tower Hamlets

  • Every wall showing damp, and the walls either side of it for comparison
  • Rainwater goods and roof junctions from the ground and from windows, and closer where access is safe
  • Basement and lower ground floors for water table, drainage and bridging at the lightwell
  • Condensation risk measured with temperature and humidity readings
  • Visible timber for decay and beetle, with moisture content recorded
  • Anything that would need opening up, identified and agreed with you rather than done unannounced

Tower Hamlets questions

Our flat is two years old and has mould. How is that possible?

Very easily, and it is usually a ventilation failure rather than anything to do with how you live. A modern flat is built airtight, so it depends entirely on mechanical ventilation to remove moisture. If that system was never commissioned, was set to a token rate, has crushed or disconnected ducting, or has been turned off because of noise, the moisture a household generates stays in the flat. We measure the actual air flow against what the design required, which is the evidence a developer, freeholder or warranty provider will respond to.

Is a report from you usable in a disrepair claim?

Yes, and it is written on the assumption that it will be read by somebody looking for holes in it. That means calibrated readings recorded by location, the mould pattern mapped against the structure, ventilation performance measured rather than described, and the external condition that explains the entry point. We carry out no remedial work, so we have no interest in what the remedy turns out to be, which is the point of instructing us rather than a contractor.

Our Wapping warehouse flat has damp on an outside wall. Why?

Thick brick walls take in rain, and if the conversion sealed the inner face they cannot dry. The survey looks at the wall build up and the external condition.

Our tower flat has mould. Is it a building defect?

It can be, if ventilation is not working or a detail lets water in. We measure and report the cause.

Do you survey for social housing tenants in Tower Hamlets?

Yes. Social landlords must investigate and act on damp and mould within fixed timescales under Awaab's Law, and an independent report helps both sides.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Tower Hamlets?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
