Catford housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The Corbett Estate, spreading across Hither Green and Catford, is Edwardian terraced housing on a large scale: two storeys, bay windows, decorative porches and a back addition, repeated with small variations across dozens of streets. Because the houses are so alike, the differences between them are what matter. One has had its suspended timber floor replaced with concrete, another a kitchen extension across the airbricks, a third a rendered front that holds water. Those changes, not the original build, usually explain the damp.

Bellingham is an interwar estate of cottage style houses and short terraces, many with rendered or roughcast walls, built with cavity or thin solid walls depending on the type. Render that has cracked and been painted over many times traps water behind it, and the small rooms and low ceilings make condensation easy to start in winter. Many of the houses have been bought and altered since, so no two are now quite the same.

Along the Ravensbourne and the Pool River the ground is lower and wetter, and gardens backing onto the river corridor can stay damp for long spells. Floors, cellars and garden walls near the water need reading on their own terms, separately from the same house type a few streets uphill.

Which kind of damp is it?

In a London building the same stain can have four or five different causes, and in Catford we regularly find two at once. Water rising from the ground through a wall with no working course. Rain entering through a parapet, a gutter, a cracked render or a window head. Moisture from the air condensing on a cold wall. Water from a pipe, a bath seal or a flat upstairs. Each needs a different repair and, in a shared building, often a different person to pay for it.

Our readings are taken to separate them: at depth as well as at the surface, at heights up the wall, on either side of the damp, and alongside humidity and surface temperatures. The pattern, not a single number, is what the diagnosis rests on.

What an ATi survey in Catford includes

  • Moisture readings taken on a grid and at height bands, so the report can show which way the water is moving
  • The outside of the building, including front areas, lightwells, parapets and rear additions where access allows
  • Communal elements that affect your flat: shared gutters, soil stacks, roof outlets and the party walls
  • Humidity, air and surface temperatures where mould or condensation is in question
  • Extract fans checked for where they discharge and how much air they move
  • Timbers within reach, and the sub floor where a hatch or a lifted board allows

Catford questions

Our Corbett Estate house has damp along the front wall. Is it rising damp?

Check the obvious first. The bay roof, the porch canopy, the front path and the airbricks under the bay are all close to that wall. Paths and front gardens have often been raised over the years, and blocked airbricks leave the timber floor without air. We record readings and the levels outside so the report shows the actual cause.

Why does our Bellingham house get mould every winter?

Small rooms, thin or uninsulated walls in places and modern living produce a lot of moisture in a small space. Cracked render can also make a wall wetter and colder than it should be. We measure surface temperatures, humidity and any extraction, and look at the walls outside, so the report separates a building fault from the way the house is used.

We back onto the river. Should we worry about flooding damp?

Living near the river does not mean a house is wet, but the ground is lower and can stay saturated after heavy rain. We look at the floor construction, any cellar, the garden levels and drainage, and record what we find so you can see whether ground water is a factor for that house.

Our previous owner put in a concrete floor. Could that cause damp?

It can. A solid floor laid into a house built with a timber floor can push moisture into the base of the walls if the two are not properly joined to a damp proof course. We check where the floor meets the wall and whether the remaining timber floors still have ventilation.

Do you recommend chemical damp proofing?

Only when the evidence supports it, which is rare in these houses. The report sets out the cause and what is needed in enough detail for any contractor to price, and we have no remedial work of our own to sell.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Catford?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
