Independent damp surveys in Sidcup

Sidcup grew around its station from the late Victorian years onward and then filled out between the wars, so the centre has older villas and terraces and the surrounding roads are almost all 1930s semis and bungalows. To the east the land drops to the River Cray at Foots Cray, and that low ground behaves differently from the rest of the town.

Sidcup housing, and what it does when it gets wet

Blackfen, Lamorbey and the roads north of the station are interwar suburb: semi detached houses with bays, hipped roofs and a mix of brick, pebbledash and render, and plenty of bungalows and chalet bungalows among them. Most have had a kitchen extension, a conservatory, a loft conversion or all three. The joins between the original house and each addition, where a flat roof meets a wall or a new floor meets an old one, are where much of the damp in this stock begins.

Around the station and the older parts of the town there are Victorian and Edwardian villas and terraces, some now flats, with solid walls, timber ground floors and older roofs. These are fewer in number but bring the problems of older construction: cement repairs to soft brick, chimney breasts that have been opened or blocked, and cellars or under floor voids with little air.

Foots Cray sits on the valley floor by the river and the meadows, with older cottages and post-war housing on lower, wetter ground. Gardens there can hold water through the winter, and houses close to the river need their floors and drainage looked at with that in mind rather than by the rules for the higher ground around Lamorbey and Blackfen.

Rising, penetrating, condensation or a leak

A tide mark low on a Sidcup wall is often called rising damp on sight. It may be. It may equally be a patio laid against the outside, a front area drain that has stopped draining, salts left behind by an old leak, or condensation collecting behind furniture on a cold wall. A pin meter cannot tell these apart, because salts and surface moisture make it read high regardless.

The survey works through the possibilities in order and records why each was accepted or ruled out, so that whoever reads the report, a solicitor, a freeholder or a contractor, can follow the reasoning.

What an ATi survey in Sidcup includes

  • Moisture readings taken on a grid and at height bands, so the report can show which way the water is moving
  • The outside of the building, including front areas, lightwells, parapets and rear additions where access allows
  • Communal elements that affect your flat: shared gutters, soil stacks, roof outlets and the party walls
  • Humidity, air and surface temperatures where mould or condensation is in question
  • Extract fans checked for where they discharge and how much air they move
  • Timbers within reach, and the sub floor where a hatch or a lifted board allows

Sidcup questions

Our Blackfen semi has damp where the extension joins the house. What causes that?

Usually the junction itself. A flat roof meeting the old wall, flashing that has pulled away, a gutter discharging at the join or the extension floor bridging the old damp proof course are the common causes. We check each with readings and an inspection outside, and the report says which applies.

Why is the bedroom in our chalet bungalow mouldy?

Rooms built into the roof have sloping ceilings and short walls with cold spots where insulation is thin or missing, and they often have little ventilation. Moisture condenses on those cold areas first. We measure surface temperatures and humidity to show where and why, so the report can separate a building fault from how the room is used.

We live near the river at Foots Cray. Is our damp ground water?

It may be part of it on the lowest ground, especially in solid floors or anything below ground. The survey separates ground water from rain, leaks and condensation, which can look similar.

Our pebbledash is cracked. Does that matter?

It can. Cracks let rain in behind the coating and the hard finish slows it drying out, so the wall behind stays wet. We look at the condition of the coating, take readings inside and record where the water is getting in.

Do you survey houses for buyers in Sidcup?

Yes. A buyer's report sets out any damp or timber problems, their cause and what is needed, in enough detail for a contractor to price before you commit.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Sidcup?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
