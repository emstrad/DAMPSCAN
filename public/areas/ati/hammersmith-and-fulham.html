Hammersmith and Fulham housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The standard Fulham terrace is solid brick, narrow fronted, with a rear closet wing and a slate roof. Side return infills are near universal, and the junction between a new flat roof and old brickwork is the single most productive defect in the borough. A flashing chased in too shallow, an upstand below the minimum, or an outlet that ponds will show internally as a damp patch on a first floor wall, some distance from where the water is actually getting in.

Basement digs are common here, generally shallower than in Kensington but numerous. They are cavity drainage systems with a perimeter channel and a pump, and they fail in the maintainable parts far more often than in the membrane. Owners are rarely told at handover that the system requires servicing, so the first failure typically arrives several years later with no maintenance history at all.

The other constant is ground level at the rear. Successive patios and garden works have raised the external ground against the back wall on a great many of these houses, bridging a damp proof course that is otherwise intact. It produces low level damp on the internal face that is repeatedly diagnosed as rising and treated accordingly.

Rising, penetrating, condensation or a leak

A tide mark low on a Hammersmith and Fulham wall is often called rising damp on sight. It may be. It may equally be a patio laid against the outside, a front area drain that has stopped draining, salts left behind by an old leak, or condensation collecting behind furniture on a cold wall. A pin meter cannot tell these apart, because salts and surface moisture make it read high regardless.

The survey works through the possibilities in order and records why each was accepted or ruled out, so that whoever reads the report, a solicitor, a freeholder or a contractor, can follow the reasoning.

Hammersmith and Fulham questions

We have been quoted for an injected damp proof course. Is it likely to be right?

Sometimes, and in this borough usually not. An injected course treats water rising through masonry from below. If the moisture is arriving from a failed side return flashing, a bridged rear ground level or a blocked gutter, injecting the wall changes nothing and the damp returns once the new plaster has dried out. We survey independently and carry out no remedial work, so we have nothing to gain either way, and we will say plainly when the work quoted for is not justified.

Our basement was dug out years ago and has started leaking. What usually causes that?

Almost always the maintainable parts rather than the waterproofing. Perimeter drainage channels silt up, sump pumps fail or their float switches stick, and service penetrations that were sealed correctly can be disturbed by later work. Because most owners are never told the system needs servicing, the first fault arrives with no maintenance history behind it. We inspect the whole system, including the parts a re waterproofing quote will not mention.

Our Fulham basement extension leaks. Does it need re tanking?

Usually not. Pumps, channels and float switches fail long before membranes. The survey checks the whole system.

Our riverside flat in Hammersmith has a damp floor. Is it the Thames?

On low ground near the river it can be groundwater. The survey separates causes.

Do you survey mansion blocks in Hammersmith?

Yes. The report traces damp to its source in the building.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Hammersmith and Fulham?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
