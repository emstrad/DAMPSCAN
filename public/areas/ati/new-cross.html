Independent damp surveys in New Cross and Deptford

New Cross and Deptford run from Telegraph Hill down to the Thames and Deptford Creek, and the buildings cover nearly three centuries: early Georgian houses in Deptford, Victorian terraces around New Cross, large post-war estates and a wave of new flats by the creek and the river. New Cross and Deptford housing, and what it does when it gets wet

Deptford has some of the oldest houses in this part of London, including early eighteenth century terraces near St Paul's Church and the High Street. These are soft brick and lime mortar with timber floors and roofs, and later cement render, cement pointing and plastic paint have sealed in moisture the walls used to lose. Damp in old walls like these is usually about what has been done to them since, and the right repair keeps them breathing.

Around New Cross, Hatcham and Telegraph Hill the streets are Victorian terraces and larger houses, many divided into flats and house shares for students and young renters near the university. Overcrowded or poorly ventilated rooms, bathrooms squeezed into small spaces and kitchens without working extraction produce condensation and mould, while the shared roofs, gutters and back additions bring leaks that cross from one flat to another.

Close to the river and the creek the ground is low, made up of river deposits, and the post-war estates and new blocks stand on it. The estates bring concrete and cold bridging; the new blocks bring sealed construction, balconies and podium gardens over car parks, each of which can leak into the flat beneath. Ground floors and anything below ground here sit near a high water table.

Which kind of damp is it?

In a London building the same stain can have four or five different causes, and in New Cross and Deptford we regularly find two at once. Water rising from the ground through a wall with no working course. Rain entering through a parapet, a gutter, a cracked render or a window head. Moisture from the air condensing on a cold wall. Water from a pipe, a bath seal or a flat upstairs. Each needs a different repair and, in a shared building, often a different person to pay for it.

Our readings are taken to separate them: at depth as well as at the surface, at heights up the wall, on either side of the damp, and alongside humidity and surface temperatures. The pattern, not a single number, is what the diagnosis rests on.

The inspection, around New Cross and across New Cross and Deptford

  • Every wall showing damp, and the walls either side of it for comparison
  • Rainwater goods and roof junctions from the ground and from windows, and closer where access is safe

New Cross and Deptford questions

I rent a room in a shared house in New Cross and it is mouldy. Can you help?

Yes. We survey for tenants and landlords alike. We measure humidity, surface temperatures and how well the extraction works, look for leaks and defects, and record whether the mould follows a cold wall, a leak or the way the room is used. The report is independent, so both sides can rely on it.

Our Deptford house is Georgian. Should the walls be injected?

Almost never. Old soft brick and lime walls need to lose moisture to the air, and the usual problem is that cement render, pointing or impervious paint has stopped them doing so, or that ground and drainage have changed around them. The report explains what is holding the moisture in and what repair suits the building, and flags where listed building consent may be needed.

Water is coming into our new flat from the balcony above. Who is responsible?

It depends on the lease and the warranty, but the first step is showing where the water comes in. We trace the route with readings and an inspection of the details above, so the report can go to the managing agent, the developer or a warranty provider with the evidence.

Is the ground near the creek a problem for damp?

It can be for floors and anything below ground, because the land is low and the water table is high. Above the ground floor it rarely matters. We look at the floor, any basement and the drainage, and say whether ground water is a factor for that property.

Do you carry out the repairs?

No. We are an independent survey practice. The report specifies what is needed so any contractor can price it, and we have no interest in which repair you choose.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in New Cross and Deptford?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
