Highbury housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The mid Victorian villas off Highbury New Park, Highbury Hill and the streets near the Fields were built for one family and servants, with a semi basement under a raised ground floor reached by front steps. The steps, the bridge over the front area and the area walls themselves are where much of the damp starts: the stone or brick steps hold water against the house, the area gully silts up, and the vaults under the pavement or garden path stay wet all winter.

Canonbury is older and more uniform: late Georgian and Regency terraces and squares in stock brick and stucco, with butterfly roofs hidden behind parapets and a central gutter running front to back through the roof space. That single gutter, and the outlet through the front parapet, is the weak point of the whole house. When it blocks, the water goes into the top floor ceiling or down inside the party wall, and the flat that finds it is often two floors below.

The ground here is London clay, which shrinks in a dry summer and swells again in winter, and Highbury has a lot of large plane and lime trees. Old cracks reopen, render and pointing move, and a joint that was weathertight last year lets rain through the next. The conversions add their own pattern: bathrooms stacked in what were bedrooms, new soil pipes boxed into old walls, and extract fans run into floor voids because the facade could not be touched.

Getting the diagnosis right in a London building

London's housing has been altered more than most: basements dug, lofts converted, houses split into flats, gardens raised, chimneys removed. In Highbury that means damp is frequently the result of a change made decades ago rather than of the original building, and the remedy is to undo or correct that change.

We look for those alterations as much as for the damp itself. A floor laid above an old course, a fan ducted into a void, a lightwell filled with decking or a stack capped without ventilation explains more damp than any failed damp proof course.

The inspection, around Highbury Fields and across Highbury

  • Every wall showing damp, and the walls either side of it for comparison
  • Rainwater goods and roof junctions from the ground and from windows, and closer where access is safe
  • Basement and lower ground floors for water table, drainage and bridging at the lightwell
  • Condensation risk measured with temperature and humidity readings
  • Visible timber for decay and beetle, with moisture content recorded
  • Anything that would need opening up, identified and agreed with you rather than done unannounced

Highbury questions

Our semi basement flat in Highbury is damp under the front window. Is that rising damp?

Usually not. The wall under the front window of a raised ground floor house sits against the front area and often behind the vaults under the steps. If the area gully is blocked, the paving falls towards the house or the vaults are wet, the wall takes water in from the side. We check those first, with readings at different heights and depths, so the report says which it is and why.

Water came through our top floor ceiling in Canonbury after heavy rain. Where from?

In a Georgian house with a butterfly roof, the first suspect is the central valley gutter and its outlet through the parapet. Leaves, a failed lining or a blocked hopper send the water straight into the roof space. We trace the route and record it, so the freeholder or roofer knows exactly where to look.

Does the conservation area change what you recommend?

It changes what can be done to the front of the house, not the diagnosis. We write the report knowing that external changes may need consent, and say where that is likely.

Can clay movement cause damp?

Indirectly. Movement opens cracks in brickwork, render and pointing, and rain then gets in through them. The report notes cracking we see and whether it lines up with the damp.

Do you survey flats in the former Highbury stadium development and other newer blocks?

Yes. Newer blocks bring different faults, usually condensation, balcony and window details or plumbing leaks, and we survey for those in the same way.

We are buying a Highbury conversion. Can the report help before exchange?

Yes. We often survey for buyers whose mortgage survey mentioned damp. The report sets out the cause and what is needed, so any contractor can price it before you commit.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Highbury?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
