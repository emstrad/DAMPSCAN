Independent damp surveys in Barnet

Barnet is mostly interwar suburb on London clay, with one important exception: Hampstead Garden Suburb, which is an Arts and Crafts conservation area governed by covenants as well as by planning. What can be done to a house there is unusually restricted, and a report that ignores that is not much use.

Barnet housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The Garden Suburb houses are roughcast rendered or brick, built to a designed aesthetic that the Trust and the conservation area exist to preserve. External alterations, replacement windows and anything visible are tightly controlled, and the original roughcast is a lime based, permeable finish. Where it has been patched or overcoated in cement or modern masonry paint, the wall stops drying, and the damp that follows is routinely misattributed. The remedy has to be permeable and it has to be approvable.

The wider borough, through Finchley, Mill Hill, Whetstone and Barnet itself, is cavity walled interwar and post-war semi with a working damp proof course. Ground level is the recurring cause: eighty years of resurfaced drives and patios have raised the external ground above the course on a great many of these houses. The symptom looks exactly like rising damp and the fix is drainage.

The clay subsoil is worth noting because of what it does to drainage runs rather than to walls directly. Clay shrinks and swells seasonally, and mature trees are common across the borough, so displaced or fractured drains under solid floors are more frequent here than in the chalk boroughs. A cracked drain produces persistent, localised damp that responds to nothing done to the wall above it.

What we are usually called out to in Barnet

  • Cement patching and modern masonry paint over permeable roughcast render in the Garden Suburb
  • Ground levels raised above the damp proof course after successive drives and patios

Which kind of damp is it?

In a London building the same stain can have four or five different causes, and in Barnet we regularly find two at once. Water rising from the ground through a wall with no working course. Rain entering through a parapet, a gutter, a cracked render or a window head. Moisture from the air condensing on a cold wall. Water from a pipe, a bath seal or a flat upstairs. Each needs a different repair and, in a shared building, often a different person to pay for it.

Our readings are taken to separate them: at depth as well as at the surface, at heights up the wall, on either side of the damp, and alongside humidity and surface temperatures. The pattern, not a single number, is what the diagnosis rests on.

How we survey a Barnet property

  • Externally first: the elevations, the ground or paving against them, and every route water has to the wall
  • For flats, the parts of the building outside the demise that can be seen or reasonably reached
  • Readings that distinguish surface salts from moisture in depth, rather than a single pin meter figure
  • Dew point conditions in the affected rooms, logged and compared with the coldest surfaces
  • Ventilation measured, not assumed from the presence of a fan
  • Previous damp treatments identified, with whether they addressed the cause

Leaseholders, landlords and buyers in Barnet

Leaseholders in Barnet often discover that the cause of their damp sits in a part of the building they do not own. Our report says where the water is coming from and which element of the building it passes through, which is the evidence a freeholder or managing agent needs under the lease. Barnet questions

We are in Hampstead Garden Suburb. Does that change the survey?

It changes what can be recommended, which is most of the value. The Suburb is both a conservation area and subject to a scheme of management, so external alterations are controlled more tightly than almost anywhere else in outer London. It also means the original render is a permeable finish that has often been overcoated with something that is not. We survey with the constraint in mind and recommend repairs that are both correct for the fabric and capable of being approved.

The damp keeps coming back in one spot on a solid floor. What causes that?

A persistent, localised damp patch on a ground floor that does not respond to anything done to the walls very often means water below the slab, and on this borough's clay the usual reason is a fractured or displaced drain. Clay moves seasonally and mature tree roots find joints. It is worth lifting the manhole covers and checking the runs before anyone specifies a floor treatment, because a treatment over a live leak will simply fail again.

Does London clay make damp worse in Barnet?

It can. Clay drains slowly, so gardens stay wet and water lingers against walls, especially where ground levels have risen. It also moves with the seasons, which can crack drains. The survey checks ground levels and drainage first.

Do we need the Trust's consent to fix damp in Hampstead Garden Suburb?

Many external changes need consent from the Trust and the council. The report flags where that is likely, and favours repairs that are generally acceptable, such as like for like rainwater and drainage work.

Our bungalow in Edgware has mould on the ceilings. Why?

Often cold spots at the ceiling edge where loft insulation stops short of the eaves, together with humidity. Measurements show how much is the building and what will help.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Barnet?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
