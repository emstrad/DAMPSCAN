Brent housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The Victorian half is solid brick, subdivided, and dependent on shared roofs, stacks and rear extensions that no single owner controls. The recurring finding is water entering high in the building and appearing in a flat that owns none of the defect, which turns a repair into a negotiation. Rear closet wings with shared valley gutters are the usual source.

The interwar half is a different world. Cavity walls, a slate or bitumen damp proof course that generally still works, airbricks feeding a void under a suspended timber floor. Damp in one of these houses is almost never rising, and when it appears at low level the cause is normally that the drive, path or patio has been built up above the damp proof course over the decades. That is a spade and a drainage channel, not a chemical treatment.

Retrofit cavity wall insulation is widespread across the Metroland stock and is a genuine cause worth checking rather than assuming. Fill that has slumped leaves the top of the wall cold, and fill that bridges the cavity carries water from the outer leaf to the inner one. The signature is damp on the most weather exposed elevation appearing after driving rain rather than continuously.

Which kind of damp is it?

In a London building the same stain can have four or five different causes, and in Brent we regularly find two at once. Water rising from the ground through a wall with no working course. Rain entering through a parapet, a gutter, a cracked render or a window head. Moisture from the air condensing on a cold wall. Water from a pipe, a bath seal or a flat upstairs. Each needs a different repair and, in a shared building, often a different person to pay for it.

Our readings are taken to separate them: at depth as well as at the surface, at heights up the wall, on either side of the damp, and alongside humidity and surface temperatures. The pattern, not a single number, is what the diagnosis rests on.

The inspection, around Wembley and across Brent

  • Every wall showing damp, and the walls either side of it for comparison
  • Rainwater goods and roof junctions from the ground and from windows, and closer where access is safe
  • Basement and lower ground floors for water table, drainage and bridging at the lightwell
  • Condensation risk measured with temperature and humidity readings
  • Visible timber for decay and beetle, with moisture content recorded
  • Anything that would need opening up, identified and agreed with you rather than done unannounced

Brent questions

Can a 1930s semi have rising damp?

It is possible and it is uncommon, because houses of that age were built with a physical damp proof course that usually still works. Far more often in this borough the course has been bridged: the drive, path or patio has been resurfaced repeatedly over eighty years until the ground sits above it, so water crosses the barrier instead of being stopped by it. The symptoms look identical from inside. The remedies do not, and one costs a great deal more than the other.

Could our cavity wall insulation be causing the damp?

It can be, and it should be checked rather than assumed either way. Retrofit fill can settle over time and leave the upper part of the wall uninsulated, or bridge the cavity so water crosses from the outer leaf to the inner one. The pattern is telling: damp on the elevation that takes the weather, appearing after driving rain and drying between, rather than constant damp at low level. We inspect for it directly and the report says what we found.

Our Kilburn lower ground flat is damp. Whose problem is it?

It depends on the lease and source. Front areas, external walls and shared drains are often the freeholder's.

Our new Wembley flat has mould. Is it a defect?

It can be, if ventilation is not working. The report measures it.

Do you survey for landlords in Brent?

Yes. The report separates building defects from condensation.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Brent?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
