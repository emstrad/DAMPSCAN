Independent damp surveys in Whitechapel

Whitechapel and Stepney hold almost every kind of London housing in a small space: early Georgian houses built for silk weavers on the Spitalfields side, Victorian tenement blocks put up by housing charities, large council estates, warehouse and factory conversions, and new towers along Whitechapel Road. Each fails in its own way, and the right diagnosis depends on knowing which one you are standing in.

Whitechapel housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The eighteenth century houses around Spitalfields and Brick Lane were built by and for the silk weaving trade, many with wide windows in the top storey to light the looms. They have thin brick walls by later standards, timber lintels and bressummers buried in the brickwork, panelled rooms and cellars. Where these houses have been restored with lime they generally behave; where cement pointing, gypsum plaster or a sealed cellar floor have gone in, moisture builds up in the brick and the hidden timbers are the first to suffer.

Across Whitechapel and Stepney there are tall Victorian and Edwardian tenement blocks built by philanthropic trusts and the LCC, with balcony or stair access, solid brick walls and flat or shallow roofs. Most are now leasehold and rented flats. Their damp usually comes from above or alongside: flat roofs and asphalt walkways, internal rainwater pipes, and cold solid walls that grow mould behind wardrobes in flats with no extract at all.

Stepney was heavily damaged in the war and rebuilt with large council estates, so much of E1 is post war concrete and brick blocks of every height. Cold bridges at balconies and slab edges, panel joints and tired windows are the recurring faults. The ground is river terrace gravel over clay, which drains well, so damp at the bottom of these buildings is more often plumbing, drainage or condensation than water from the ground.

Getting the diagnosis right in a London building

London's housing has been altered more than most: basements dug, lofts converted, houses split into flats, gardens raised, chimneys removed. In Whitechapel that means damp is frequently the result of a change made decades ago rather than of the original building, and the remedy is to undo or correct that change.

We look for those alterations as much as for the damp itself. A floor laid above an old course, a fan ducted into a void, a lightwell filled with decking or a stack capped without ventilation explains more damp than any failed damp proof course.

What an ATi survey in Whitechapel includes

  • Moisture readings taken on a grid and at height bands, so the report can show which way the water is moving
  • The outside of the building, including front areas, lightwells, parapets and rear additions where access allows
  • Communal elements that affect your flat: shared gutters, soil stacks, roof outlets and the party walls
  • Humidity, air and surface temperatures where mould or condensation is in question
  • Extract fans checked for where they discharge and how much air they move
  • Timbers within reach, and the sub floor where a hatch or a lifted board allows

Whitechapel questions

We own a Georgian house near Spitalfields. Should a damp survey worry about the timber?

Yes, it is one of the main reasons to have one. Houses of this age have timber lintels and beams built into the brickwork, and if the wall around them stays wet they decay out of sight. We take readings at those points and look for the signs of decay, and the report says where the timber needs opening up and checking.

Our cellar was tanked and is still damp. Why?

A cellar is below ground, so the walls will always take in some water from outside. If the lining has failed at a joint, a drain is leaking or the cellar has no ventilation, it will show. We find where it is getting in and whether condensation is part of it, so the report says what is needed.

Our flat in an old tenement block has mould on every outside wall. Is that us or the building?

Often both. Solid walls are cold and many of these flats have no extract fan. We measure the conditions and record which part is the building and which is use.

Do you survey council and housing association flats in E1?

Yes, for tenants, leaseholders and landlords. The report is written with measurements and photographs so both sides can rely on it.

Our converted warehouse flat has damp behind the plasterboard. How do you find it?

We look at how the wall is built up and take readings through and behind the lining where needed, so the report says whether it is rain getting in or condensation inside the wall.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Whitechapel?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
