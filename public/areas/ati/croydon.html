Croydon housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The classic Croydon house is a bay fronted semi from the twenties or thirties, cavity walled, with airbricks at low level feeding a void under a suspended timber ground floor. Those two features do most of the work in keeping the house dry, and both are routinely defeated. Airbricks get rendered over, buried by a raised patio, or blocked by a new conservatory. Cavities get filled with retrofit insulation that slumps or bridges. Both produce damp at low level on an internal wall face, which looks exactly like rising damp and is not.

Ground levels are the other recurring theme. Sixty or seventy years of successive driveways, patios and block paving have raised the external ground on a great many of these houses above the damp proof course. Once the ground is above the DPC the wall is bridged and the course is irrelevant, however good it is. The fix is a spade and a drainage channel, not a chemical.

The town centre brings a different stock again: post-war blocks, sixties towers and a significant number of recent office to residential conversions. Those conversions were often done under permitted development with limited scope for changing the building envelope, and single aspect flats with mechanical ventilation and large areas of glazing produce persistent condensation that is a design outcome rather than a resident's doing.

What we are usually called out to in Croydon

Rising, penetrating, condensation or a leak

A tide mark low on a Croydon wall is often called rising damp on sight. It may be. It may equally be a patio laid against the outside, a front area drain that has stopped draining, salts left behind by an old leak, or condensation collecting behind furniture on a cold wall. A pin meter cannot tell these apart, because salts and surface moisture make it read high regardless.

The survey works through the possibilities in order and records why each was accepted or ruled out, so that whoever reads the report, a solicitor, a freeholder or a contractor, can follow the reasoning.

What an ATi survey in Croydon includes

  • Moisture readings taken on a grid and at height bands, so the report can show which way the water is moving
  • The outside of the building, including front areas, lightwells, parapets and rear additions where access allows
  • Communal elements that affect your flat: shared gutters, soil stacks, roof outlets and the party walls
  • Humidity, air and surface temperatures where mould or condensation is in question
  • Extract fans checked for where they discharge and how much air they move
  • Timbers within reach, and the sub floor where a hatch or a lifted board allows

Croydon questions

Can a 1930s house get rising damp?

It is possible but uncommon, because houses of that age were built with a physical damp proof course that generally still works. What is far more common in Croydon is that the course has been bridged: the drive, patio or garden has been built up above it over the decades, so water crosses the barrier rather than being stopped by it. The symptoms are identical. The remedies are not, and one of them costs a great deal more than the other.

We have had cavity wall insulation. Could that be the cause?

It can be, and it is worth checking rather than assuming. Retrofit cavity fill can slump over time and leave the top of the wall uninsulated, or bridge the cavity and carry water from the outer leaf to the inner one. The signature is damp patches on the most weather exposed elevation, often at first floor level, appearing after driving rain rather than continuously. We inspect for it directly rather than inferring it, and the report says what we found.

Our Croydon semi smells musty under the floor. What causes that?

Usually a suspended timber floor with blocked airbricks, often behind a later path or extension. Reopening and adding ventilation is the main repair, with any decayed timber replaced.

We are buying in Purley and the house is on a slope. What should we check?

Retaining walls, drainage on the uphill side and any rooms below ground level. The survey covers these and the report says what they need.

Do you survey flats above shops in central Croydon?

Yes. Damp in flats above shops often comes from parapets, flat roofs and shared drainage, and the report identifies the source and likely responsibility.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Croydon?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
