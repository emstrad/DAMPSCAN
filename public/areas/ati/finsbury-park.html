Independent damp surveys in Finsbury Park

Finsbury Park sits where three boroughs meet, and the housing changes every few streets: tall late Victorian terraces climbing the hill to Stroud Green, the tighter streets off Blackstock Road, the edge of the Harringay grid along Green Lanes, and large estates towards Manor House. Finsbury Park housing, and what it does when it gets wet

Stroud Green was built up quickly in the last decades of the nineteenth century on the slope rising towards Crouch Hill. The houses are three storeys or more with a deep rear wing, and many back gardens sit higher than the floor of the rear rooms because the streets were cut into the hill. A ground floor flat at the back of one of these houses is often below the garden on one side, and the rear wall behaves more like a retaining wall than a house wall.

The terraces near the station and along Blackstock Road and Stroud Green Road include a lot of flats over shops. Flat roofs over the rear of the shop, a back yard covered over years ago and a parapet gutter shared along the parade are typical sources of water for the flat above. The shop and the flat often have different owners, which is why the cause needs pinning down in writing.

Towards Manor House and Woodberry Down the stock turns to post war council blocks, some still standing and many replaced by the newer flats of the regeneration around the reservoirs. The older blocks show cold corners and mould where the structure carries heat out; the newer ones are well insulated and airtight, so when the mechanical ventilation is switched off or blocked, condensation builds quickly.

Rising, penetrating, condensation or a leak

A tide mark low on a Finsbury Park wall is often called rising damp on sight. It may be. It may equally be a patio laid against the outside, a front area drain that has stopped draining, salts left behind by an old leak, or condensation collecting behind furniture on a cold wall. A pin meter cannot tell these apart, because salts and surface moisture make it read high regardless.

The survey works through the possibilities in order and records why each was accepted or ruled out, so that whoever reads the report, a solicitor, a freeholder or a contractor, can follow the reasoning.

The inspection, around Finsbury Park and across Finsbury Park

  • Every wall showing damp, and the walls either side of it for comparison
  • Rainwater goods and roof junctions from the ground and from windows, and closer where access is safe
  • Basement and lower ground floors for water table, drainage and bridging at the lightwell
  • Condensation risk measured with temperature and humidity readings
  • Visible timber for decay and beetle, with moisture content recorded
  • Anything that would need opening up, identified and agreed with you rather than done unannounced

Leaseholders, landlords and buyers in Finsbury Park

Leaseholders in Finsbury Park often discover that the cause of their damp sits in a part of the building they do not own. Our report says where the water is coming from and which element of the building it passes through, which is the evidence a freeholder or managing agent needs under the lease. For landlords, damp and mould are hazards under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System, and social landlords in England now work to the fixed timescales of Awaab's Law. Finsbury Park questions

The back bedroom of our Stroud Green ground floor flat is damp. The garden is higher than the floor. Is that the cause?

It is the first thing we check. Where ground outside is higher than the floor inside, water reaches the wall sideways, and no damp proof course was ever meant to stop that. We measure levels inside and out and take readings across the wall, so the report shows whether the damp follows the ground line. It then sets out what is needed for the wall to stay dry.

Our flat is over a shop and the ceiling is stained. Who is responsible?

That depends on where the water is coming from and on the leases. We trace the route, whether it is the shop's roof, a parade gutter or something inside your own flat, and record it with photographs and readings. That is what the freeholder or the shop owner will need to see.

Our new build flat near Woodberry Down has mould. Is it a defect?

Sometimes. We check the ventilation unit, the extract rates and the surface temperatures, and record whether the mould sits where the building is cold or where the air is wet.

Do you survey for buyers in N4?

Yes, often after a mortgage survey has flagged damp. The report is written so that any contractor can price what is needed.

Can you survey a whole converted house for the freeholder?

Yes. Surveying every flat at once usually shows where the shared defects are and which damp belongs to which flat.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Finsbury Park?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
