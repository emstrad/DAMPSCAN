Independent damp surveys in Peckham

Peckham is mostly Victorian: long terraced streets either side of Rye Lane, bigger houses near the Rye and the park, and a hillier, quieter grid of terraces in Nunhead. Many have been split into flats and many more are being turned back into family houses, with loft rooms, kitchen extensions and basements dug out. A lot of the damp we see here comes from the joins between old and new work.

Peckham housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The streets around Bellenden Road and towards the Rye are mainly two and three storey terraces from the 1870s to the 1890s, solid brick with a rear wing, a bay at the front and suspended timber floors. The common modern alteration is a side return extension with a glass or flat roof. Where it meets the old rear wing, the flashing, the gutter between the two roofs and the wall the extension is built against all have to be right, and when one of them is not the water appears on the new kitchen wall.

Nunhead rises towards the cemetery and One Tree Hill, and its streets climb with it. Houses on the slope often have gardens above the floor at the back, and the houses lower down take the water coming off the hill. Ground floor rooms against a sloping garden behave differently from those on flat ground, and the readings usually show it.

Peckham also has large council estates, much of North Peckham rebuilt from the 1990s on, alongside older blocks still standing, and a growing number of flats in converted industrial buildings off Rye Lane. The ground is London clay, and with big trees along the Rye and in the park, movement cracks in brickwork and render are common. Rain getting into those cracks is a regular cause of damp on exposed walls.

Which kind of damp is it?

In a London building the same stain can have four or five different causes, and in Peckham we regularly find two at once. Water rising from the ground through a wall with no working course. Rain entering through a parapet, a gutter, a cracked render or a window head. Moisture from the air condensing on a cold wall. Water from a pipe, a bath seal or a flat upstairs. Each needs a different repair and, in a shared building, often a different person to pay for it.

Our readings are taken to separate them: at depth as well as at the surface, at heights up the wall, on either side of the damp, and alongside humidity and surface temperatures. The pattern, not a single number, is what the diagnosis rests on.

What an ATi survey in Peckham includes

  • Moisture readings taken on a grid and at height bands, so the report can show which way the water is moving
  • The outside of the building, including front areas, lightwells, parapets and rear additions where access allows
  • Communal elements that affect your flat: shared gutters, soil stacks, roof outlets and the party walls
  • Humidity, air and surface temperatures where mould or condensation is in question
  • Extract fans checked for where they discharge and how much air they move
  • Timbers within reach, and the sub floor where a hatch or a lifted board allows

Peckham questions

Our new kitchen extension in Peckham has damp where it meets the old house. Why?

That junction is the most common source of damp we find in altered Victorian houses. A flashing that is missing or too short, a gutter between the new roof and the old wing, or an old wall left without a cavity tray will let water in. We trace it and record exactly where, so whoever puts it right knows where to start.

Is the damp in our Nunhead ground floor from the hill?

It can be. Where the garden is higher than the floor inside, water reaches the rear wall sideways. We measure the levels and take readings at different heights, so the report shows whether that is the cause or whether it is drainage, plumbing or condensation.

Our loft conversion has mould on the sloping ceiling. Is it a leak?

Sometimes, but often it is condensation where the insulation or ventilation is not right. We check both, and record surface temperatures as well as moisture readings.

Do you survey estate flats in Peckham?

Yes, for tenants, leaseholders and landlords. The report shows with measurements what is a building issue and what is not.

We are buying in SE15 and the mortgage survey says damp. What next?

An independent damp survey before exchange. The report sets out the cause and what is needed, so it can be priced by any contractor and used in your negotiation.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Peckham?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
