Brockley housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The streets of the Brockley conservation area are lined with big semi detached and terraced villas, three or four storeys with a raised ground floor and a lower ground floor half below the garden. Many were converted into flats decades ago. The lower ground flat is where trouble collects: its back wall holds up a garden that rises away from the house, its front sits behind a lightwell or a sunken area, and it is the lowest point for any leak in the building.

Crofton Park and the streets towards Honor Oak are smaller late Victorian and Edwardian terraces, two storeys with a bay at the front and a narrow back addition. Here the faults are the familiar terrace ones made worse by the hills: rear gardens and paths built up over the years, flat roofs on back additions that have outlived their covering, and gutters shared with the next house along.

The ground is London Clay, which holds water rather than letting it drain away and shrinks and swells with the seasons. With mature street trees and large back gardens, that means cracked drains, movement around bay windows and water lying against walls after rain. It also means a cellar or lower ground room is in wet ground for much of the year.

Which kind of damp is it?

In a London building the same stain can have four or five different causes, and in Brockley we regularly find two at once. Water rising from the ground through a wall with no working course. Rain entering through a parapet, a gutter, a cracked render or a window head. Moisture from the air condensing on a cold wall. Water from a pipe, a bath seal or a flat upstairs. Each needs a different repair and, in a shared building, often a different person to pay for it.

Our readings are taken to separate them: at depth as well as at the surface, at heights up the wall, on either side of the damp, and alongside humidity and surface temperatures. The pattern, not a single number, is what the diagnosis rests on.

How we survey a Brockley property

  • Externally first: the elevations, the ground or paving against them, and every route water has to the wall
  • For flats, the parts of the building outside the demise that can be seen or reasonably reached
  • Readings that distinguish surface salts from moisture in depth, rather than a single pin meter figure
  • Dew point conditions in the affected rooms, logged and compared with the coldest surfaces
  • Ventilation measured, not assumed from the presence of a fan
  • Previous damp treatments identified, with whether they addressed the cause

Brockley questions

We are buying a lower ground flat in Brockley. What should we check?

Where the walls are below the outside ground, and how water is kept away from them. We read the rear and side walls against the garden levels, look at the lightwell or front area and its drain, check whether any earlier waterproofing is in place and working, and note anything coming from the floors above. The report tells you which problems belong to the flat and which to the building.

Water is coming through our top floor ceiling. Is that our problem?

It depends on the lease, but the source is usually a roof, valley or parapet gutter that serves the whole house. Many Brockley villas have a valley roof behind a parapet that cannot be seen from the street, and a blocked outlet there sends water into the top flat. The report records where the water enters, which helps leaseholders and freeholders agree who deals with it.

Does the clay make damp worse in SE4?

It makes water slower to drain away, so anything that sends water to the base of a wall, a leaking gully, a cracked drain or a path laid towards the house, has more effect than it would on free draining ground. We look at drainage and ground levels around the house as well as the walls inside.

Our Crofton Park terrace has damp in the kitchen at the back. Where do we start?

With the back addition. Its flat roof, the gutter and downpipe it shares with next door and the level of the garden or patio against it are the usual causes. We check each of these with readings before considering anything rising from the ground.

Can you survey for a freeholder and all the leaseholders together?

Yes. We can survey the whole building or a single flat, and the report is written so that each party can see which defect affects which part of the house.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Brockley?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
