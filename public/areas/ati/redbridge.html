Redbridge housing, and what it does when it gets wet

The Ilford Edwardian terraces are solid brick with rear closet wings and slate roofs, and a great many have been extended at the rear and converted in the loft. Loft conversions are worth singling out here because they are so common and so frequently the cause: a conversion that fills the roof space removes the ventilation path the roof depended on, and unless replacement ventilation was designed in, condensation forms in the remaining void and appears as damp on a ceiling below.

The Woodford and Wanstead stock is larger interwar and inter-generational, cavity walled with working damp proof courses and often with tile hung or rendered upper elevations. Tile hanging fails invisibly and needs close inspection. Rendered elevations that have been overcoated in modern masonry paint trap water in the same way as anywhere else.

The Roding valley matters for the properties near it, where ground water and surface drainage are genuinely a factor, and for the mature tree cover across the borough, which fills gutters reliably every autumn and puts roots into old clay drainage. A displaced drain under a solid floor is a recurring finding on the older stock.

What we are usually called out to in Redbridge

Which kind of damp is it?

In a London building the same stain can have four or five different causes, and in Redbridge we regularly find two at once. Water rising from the ground through a wall with no working course. Rain entering through a parapet, a gutter, a cracked render or a window head. Moisture from the air condensing on a cold wall. Water from a pipe, a bath seal or a flat upstairs. Each needs a different repair and, in a shared building, often a different person to pay for it.

Our readings are taken to separate them: at depth as well as at the surface, at heights up the wall, on either side of the damp, and alongside humidity and surface temperatures. The pattern, not a single number, is what the diagnosis rests on.

What an ATi survey in Redbridge includes

  • Moisture readings taken on a grid and at height bands, so the report can show which way the water is moving
  • The outside of the building, including front areas, lightwells, parapets and rear additions where access allows
  • Communal elements that affect your flat: shared gutters, soil stacks, roof outlets and the party walls
  • Humidity, air and surface temperatures where mould or condensation is in question
  • Extract fans checked for where they discharge and how much air they move

Redbridge questions

We had a loft conversion and now the ceiling below is damp. Why?

Because the conversion probably removed the ventilation the roof depended on without replacing it. A traditional cold roof works by having air move freely through the space above the insulation, carrying moisture away. Fill that space with rooms and the remaining voids at the edges become cold, unventilated pockets where moisture condenses. It appears as damp on a ceiling and is very often diagnosed as a roof leak. The fix is ventilation, not a new roof covering.

Do you inspect the roof, or only the inside?

Both, and the roof is often where the answer is. Damp appearing on an upper floor or a ceiling usually originates above it, and the causes are rarely visible from the ground: slipped hanging tiles, a lifted flashing, a blocked valley, or a loft conversion that closed off a ventilation path. We take internal readings to establish where the water is arriving and then work upward and outward to find where it is entering.

Our Woodford house has damp high on the wall. Is that rising damp?

No. Damp at upper levels is usually rainwater from a gutter, hopper or valley, often blocked by leaves from mature trees. The survey traces the source.

Our Ilford conversion has damp in the lower flat. Whose problem is it?

It depends on the lease and the source. External walls, roofs and shared drains are often the freeholder's responsibility.

Do interwar houses in Redbridge need damp proofing?

Rarely. They usually have working courses. Damp at low level is normally bridging by drives, patios or extension floors.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Redbridge?

No. We carry out no remedial work, so there is nothing to sell after the survey. The report states the cause, the work that would actually fix it and an outline of what that work should reasonably cost, so you can get like for like quotes from contractors of your choice, or put it to a seller, landlord or freeholder.
