Damp survey in Ashford

Ashford is unusual in Kent for how much of its housing is new. Alongside the Victorian railway terraces around the station there are very large estates built since the 1970s, at Singleton, Park Farm, Kingsnorth, Repton Park and Godinton, and new build damp is a different subject entirely: it is about airtightness, ventilation and construction moisture rather than about anything having failed.

Ashford homes, and how they get damp

A modern house is built to be airtight and insulated to a standard the older stock never approached. That is good for heating bills and unforgiving of ventilation failures. If the mechanical ventilation was never commissioned, has been turned down because it was noisy, or has ducting that was crushed or left disconnected in the roof, the moisture a household generates has nowhere to go. Mould in a two year old house is almost always a ventilation defect, and it is a defect the builder is often still responsible for.

The other new build phenomenon is construction moisture. A newly built masonry house contains a great deal of water in its blockwork, screed and plaster, and it takes many months to dry out. During the first winter that shows as condensation, streaming windows and mould in cupboards and corners, and it is routinely misdiagnosed as a leak. Knowing the difference saves an owner from chasing a defect that is not there, and from accepting one that is.

The older parts of the town, around the station, Newtown and the original centre, are conventional Victorian brick terraces with the usual issues: raised external ground bridging the damp proof course, defective rainwater goods, blocked airbricks. Out in Kennington, Willesborough and the villages there is weatherboarding, timber frame and the occasional oast, which need breathable repairs rather than modern impermeable ones.

Ground, water and weather in Ashford

Ashford sits where the Great Stour and the East Stour meet, on Weald clay and river deposits, with the greensand rising to the north. The river valleys through the town are low and wet, and areas near the rivers have flood risk. Weald clay drains slowly and moves with the seasons, so gardens hold water and new estates rely on engineered drainage and balancing ponds.

New build warranties and what to record

Most new homes in England come with a structural warranty for ten years, and the developer is usually responsible for putting right defects reported in the first two years. Damp and mould in a new Ashford home often fall into that window, and what settles a dispute is evidence: measured ventilation flow against the design figure, humidity and surface temperature readings, photographs of ducting in the loft, and the pattern of the mould.

We record those in a form a developer's customer care team or a warranty provider can act on. Where the evidence points to how the house is used rather than how it was built, the report says that too, with practical steps, because there is no point pursuing a claim the facts will not support.

Rising damp, penetrating damp or condensation?

They can look the same on a Ashford wall: a stain, a tide mark, blown plaster or mould. They behave differently. Rising damp is steady through the year and highest at the base of a wall. Penetrating damp follows the weather and sits where water has a route in, around a window, under a gutter, on the exposed side. Condensation is worst in cold weather, on the coldest surfaces, and often comes with streaming windows.

A meter alone cannot tell them apart, and salts in old plaster make every damp meter read high. The pattern, the readings at depth, the outside of the building and the room conditions together can, which is why the survey takes the time it does.

What a DampScan survey in Ashford covers

A survey here is a morning or an afternoon with one qualified surveyor, not a salesman with a meter. In Ashford that means:

  • Moisture readings across a grid on every affected wall, not just the stain, so the shape of the damp is on record
  • A look at Victorian brick and modern block or brick outside, joint by joint where it matters, because the cause is usually on the outside face
  • Ground and path levels against each elevation, measured against the damp proof course or the internal floor
  • Gutters, downpipes, gullies and any drain covers we can lift
  • Air temperature, humidity and surface temperatures, so condensation is measured rather than guessed
  • Ventilation: whether fans extract to outside and how much air they actually move
  • Floor voids and exposed timbers where they can be reached, for rot and beetle

Buying or letting in Ashford

Many Ashford buyers are choosing between a newer house on one of the estates and a Victorian terrace near the station. On the new house, find out whether any damp is construction moisture, a ventilation fault or a defect still under warranty. On the terrace, find out whether it is ground level, rainwater or salts. An independent survey before exchange answers both.

Letting in Ashford? Damp and mould in rented homes are a hazard under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System, and social landlords in England now have fixed timescales to investigate and act under Awaab's Law, with the government planning to extend similar duties to private renting. An independent report that separates building defects from condensation is the evidence both landlords and tenants need. See our guide to landlord damp and mould duties.

Damp survey questions from Ashford

Our new build is damp. Is that normal, or is something wrong?

Both are common and need telling apart. Condensation through the first winter can be normal while construction moisture dries. Persistent mould, mould in later winters, or mould near a ventilation terminal is a defect. We measure ventilation performance as well as moisture, which is what a warranty provider will want.

The developer says it is condensation and therefore our responsibility. Is it?

Not necessarily. If it is happening because a ventilation system does not move the air it was specified to, because ducting is crushed or disconnected, or because the system was never commissioned, that is a construction defect. Our report records measured air flow against what the design required.

Our Victorian terrace near the station has damp at the front. Why?

Usually raised pavement or path levels bridging the course, or a bay or downpipe. The survey finds which.

Do you cover Kennington, Willesborough and the villages?

Yes, and Wye, Charing, Hamstreet, Tenterden and the rest of the borough.

Is our house at risk from the rivers?

Flood risk is for the Environment Agency maps and insurers. We can tell you how the building is coping with moisture and whether groundwater is affecting it.

How soon can you visit?

Same day reply and usually within a few days. Ashford is a straight run down the M20.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Ashford?

No. DampScan is a survey and report practice and carries out no remedial work of any kind. Where work is needed, the report specifies it so any contractor can price it, and where it is not, the report says so.
