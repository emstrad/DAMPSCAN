Damp survey in Broadstairs

Broadstairs is a small seaside town around Viking Bay, with its Victorian and Edwardian seafront, the older village of St Peter's inland with its flint church and cottages, and the larger houses of Kingsgate along the cliffs. It is quieter and more residential than Margate and Ramsgate, with a high proportion of older owner occupied houses and bungalows, and a coastline of chalk bays that faces the weather.

Broadstairs homes, and how they get damp

Around the harbour and the town centre, houses are Victorian and Edwardian brick and render, some older, with bays and balconies facing the sea. Exposed elevations take spray and wind driven rain, and their render and bays need constant upkeep. Salt in masonry close to the front keeps readings high long after walls have dried.

St Peter's and Reading Street have flint and brick cottages and farmhouses, some very old, built with lime and without damp proof courses. They stay dry when their mortar is lime, their gutters work and their ground is kept low. Cement repointing and raised gardens are the usual reasons they become damp.

Much of the rest of Broadstairs, towards Dumpton, Westwood and Kingsgate, is interwar and post war housing, including many bungalows. Condensation, cold walls, flat roofs and extensions are typical sources of damp.

Ground, water and weather in Broadstairs

Broadstairs sits on Thanet chalk, cut into bays along the coast, with the cliffs at Kingsgate and Stone Bay. Chalk drains quickly, so damp in cellars and against higher ground arrives with heavy rain and fades. The coastal edge takes the full force of easterly weather and spray.

Living with sea air in Broadstairs

Salt is the thing that makes damp in Broadstairs hard to read. Spray and salt laden wind deposit salts on and in masonry and plaster, and those salts are hygroscopic: they draw moisture from the air. A wall that has not had liquid water in it for years can feel cool and read high on a meter on a humid day, simply because of what is in its surface. Treating that as rising damp is one of the most common mistakes we see along this coast.

The tell is in the pattern. Salt damp tends to come and go with the humidity of the air rather than with rain, is patchy rather than forming a clear band, and shows on walls facing the sea more than on sheltered ones. Readings taken at depth, compared with surface readings, show whether there is water inside the wall or only salt on the face. That distinction decides whether anything needs doing at all, and if so, whether it is a plaster that tolerates salts or something more.

Bungalows deserve a mention of their own. Broadstairs has a great many, and they lose heat through a large area of roof and wall for the size of the home. Cold ceilings at the wall junction and cold corners are where mould appears, and the answer is usually loft insulation pushed properly to the eaves without blocking ventilation, together with extraction that works.

Rising damp, penetrating damp or condensation?

They can look the same on a Broadstairs wall: a stain, a tide mark, blown plaster or mould. They behave differently. Rising damp is steady through the year and highest at the base of a wall. Penetrating damp follows the weather and sits where water has a route in, around a window, under a gutter, on the exposed side. Condensation is worst in cold weather, on the coldest surfaces, and often comes with streaming windows.

A meter alone cannot tell them apart, and salts in old plaster make every damp meter read high. The pattern, the readings at depth, the outside of the building and the room conditions together can, which is why the survey takes the time it does.

What we check on a damp survey in Broadstairs

The visit follows the water. Whatever brought you to us, in Broadstairs we work through:

Damp survey questions from Broadstairs

Our flint cottage in St Peter's has damp patches. Does it need a damp proof course?

Probably not. Flint cottages were built without one and stay dry with lime mortar, working gutters and low ground. Cement repointing and raised gardens are the usual causes of damp.

Why does our seafront house have damp after storms?

Wind driven rain and spray get through cracked render, bays and balcony details. The survey finds the routes in.

Our bungalow has mould on the ceiling corners. Why?

Often cold spots where insulation is thin at the ceiling edge, combined with humidity. Surface temperatures and humidity readings show how much is the building.

Do you cover Kingsgate and Westwood?

Yes, and the rest of Broadstairs and Thanet.

Are meter readings reliable by the sea?

Not alone, because salt gives high readings. Readings at depth and the pattern tell the real story.

How soon can you visit?

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Broadstairs?

No. DampScan is a survey and report practice and carries out no remedial work of any kind. Where work is needed, the report specifies it so any contractor can price it, and where it is not, the report says so.
