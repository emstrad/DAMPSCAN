Damp survey in Margate

Margate is one of the oldest seaside resorts in the country, and its buildings run from the Georgian squares and the Old Town by the harbour, through the large Victorian houses of Cliftonville, many long since divided into flats, to the interwar and post war roads of Garlinge, Northdown and Palm Bay. It sits on chalk above the sea, facing north, and the combination of age, exposure and subdivision produces a particular set of damp problems.

Margate homes, and how they get damp

The Old Town and the Georgian streets near the harbour have brick, flint and rendered buildings, some with cellars cut into the chalk, many listed or in conservation areas. Lime built and exposed to salt air, they suffer when repaired in cement and sealed with modern paint, and their cellars are damp in the way chalk cellars always have been.

Cliftonville's large Victorian and Edwardian houses and terraces, built for visitors and hotels, have often been subdivided into flats and houses in multiple occupation. Parapets, valley gutters, flat roofed rear additions and shared soil stacks are frequent sources of water, and the responsibility for fixing them is split between owners, freeholders and landlords. Overcrowded or poorly ventilated flats add condensation and mould.

Further from the front, the twentieth century houses of Garlinge, Northdown and Palm Bay have cavity walls and concrete floors, and their damp is mainly condensation, ventilation and bridged courses, with exposure on the seaward elevations of houses close to the cliffs.

Ground, water and weather in Margate

Margate sits on the chalk of the Isle of Thanet, which drains quickly, with chalk cliffs along the coast. Water moves rapidly through the ground after rain, so damp that follows the weather is common in cellars and on walls against higher ground. Sea spray and salt carried inland on north and north easterly winds keep masonry close to the front damp and salty.

Flats, houses in multiple occupation and mould

A large share of Margate's older housing has been subdivided, and that changes how damp and mould behave. A house built for one family, with open fireplaces, draughty sash windows and a scullery, now holds several households in flats or rooms, each with a kitchen and shower, and the fireplaces are blocked and the windows replaced. The moisture load has gone up and the air changes have gone down.

The result is condensation and black mould in bedrooms and bathrooms, especially on outside walls and in rooms in the roof. Landlords are often told it is the tenants' fault and tenants are told to open windows. The truth is usually that the building needs extraction sized for the occupancy and heating that keeps surfaces warm, and the measurements make that clear.

Where the mould sits alongside a building defect, such as a leaking parapet, a cracked soil stack or a failed flat roof over a rear addition, the report separates the two, because one is for the freeholder or landlord to fix and the other may need everyone in the building to change how it is run.

Three kinds of damp, three different fixes

Rising damp needs the ground moisture stopped at the base of the wall. Penetrating damp needs the route in closed: a gutter, a joint, a sill, a flashing. Condensation needs less moisture in the air, more air movement and warmer surfaces. In Margate, as everywhere, these get confused constantly, and a remedy for one does nothing for the others.

That is the case for an independent survey in a sentence. Somebody needs to work out which problem you have before anybody sells you the fix for one of them.

What we check on a damp survey in Margate

The visit follows the water. Whatever brought you to us, in Margate we work through:

  • The roofline and chimney from the ground and from windows, then gutters and downpipes in detail
  • Every outside wall for cracks, open joints, failed render and anything bridging the base of the wall
  • Airbricks and sub floor ventilation, often buried by later paths and extensions
  • Internal walls with calibrated readings at height bands, so rising, penetrating and condensation damp can be told apart
  • Surface and air conditions in the problem rooms, logged rather than estimated
  • Kitchen and bathroom extraction, measured at the grille
  • Any earlier damp proofing, its paperwork and whether it was ever the right answer

Buying or letting in Margate

Margate has seen many buyers arrive in recent years, often buying flats in converted Cliftonville houses or Georgian homes needing work. Damp is common in both. An independent survey before exchange separates problems in your flat from problems in the building, which is vital when the fix depends on a freeholder or other owners.

Letting in Margate? Damp and mould in rented homes are a hazard under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System, and social landlords in England now have fixed timescales to investigate and act under Awaab's Law, with the government planning to extend similar duties to private renting. Damp survey questions from Margate

Our Cliftonville flat has damp in the top floor ceiling. Is it the roof?

Very often, through a parapet, valley gutter or flashing. In converted buildings, the roof is usually a common part, so the report identifies the source to support a request to the freeholder.

We are a landlord with mould complaints. What should we do?

Get the cause investigated promptly and in writing. Mould in flats is often a mix of building defects and occupancy; the report separates them and sets out what the landlord should fix.

Is a damp chalk cellar a problem?

Chalk cellars are often damp after heavy rain because water moves quickly through chalk. Whether that matters depends on how you use the cellar. A habitable room needs a designed below ground solution.

Do you cover Westgate, Birchington and Broadstairs?

Yes, and Ramsgate and the rest of Thanet.

Why is our seafront wall damp only in winter storms?

Wind driven rain and spray on exposed walls get through cracked render, open joints and sills. The survey finds the routes in.

How soon can you visit?

Same day reply and usually a visit within a few days.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Margate?

No. DampScan is a survey and report practice and carries out no remedial work of any kind. Where work is needed, the report specifies it so any contractor can price it, and where it is not, the report says so.
