Damp survey in Ramsgate

Ramsgate grew around its Royal Harbour, and the town that climbs the chalk on either side is largely Regency and Victorian: crescents and terraces of stucco and brick facing the sea, narrower streets of workers' houses behind, and the buildings associated with Augustus Pugin on the West Cliff. Further inland, Newington, Nethercourt and the villages towards Manston and Minster are mostly twentieth century.

Ramsgate homes, and how they get damp

The Regency crescents and terraces on the East and West Cliffs and around the harbour are brick behind painted stucco, with basements and lower ground floors cut into the chalk, and many are listed. Stucco protects the brick when it is sound; once it cracks and is painted over with impermeable paint, it traps water. Basements are often damp at the rear, where they retain the slope.

Behind the front, the streets of smaller Victorian houses are solid brick or flint and brick, with yards and rear additions. Flint walls with brick dressings are common in Thanet, and they rely on lime mortar to stay sound and dry. Cement repointing on flint is a frequent cause of damp and of loose flints.

Newington, Nethercourt and Westwood are post war and later, with cavity walls and concrete floors. Mould, condensation and ventilation are the main problems, along with flat roofed extensions and drives laid too high.

Ground, water and weather in Ramsgate

Ramsgate sits on chalk at the eastern tip of Thanet, with cliffs along the coast and Pegwell Bay to the south west, where the land drops to the Stour estuary and the marshes. The chalk drains quickly, so cellars and basements see damp that arrives with heavy rain and fades. Sea spray and salt reach well inland on easterly winds, adding salts to exposed masonry.

Basements, crescents and the slope

A good deal of Ramsgate's most attractive housing is built on the edge of the chalk where it drops to the harbour, and that shapes the damp. Crescents and terraces on the cliff tops often have lower ground floors cut into the chalk, with the street at the front and a lightwell or area below it. The front wall of the lower ground floor holds back the pavement, the vault under it and anything that drains into them.

Damp at the front of a lower ground floor is therefore frequently a pavement vault, an area drain or a broken pipe under the street, not a wall failure. Damp at the rear may be the slope. Both are lateral, and both are dealt with at the source or with a below ground system, never with a damp proof course injected along the wall.

Above ground, the stucco is the thing to watch. Painted every few years, often with modern masonry paints, it eventually stops breathing, and fine cracks let in rain that cannot get back out. The first sign is usually blown plaster inside on an upper floor of the seaward wall.

Three kinds of damp, three different fixes

Rising damp needs the ground moisture stopped at the base of the wall. Penetrating damp needs the route in closed: a gutter, a joint, a sill, a flashing. Condensation needs less moisture in the air, more air movement and warmer surfaces. In Ramsgate, as everywhere, these get confused constantly, and a remedy for one does nothing for the others.

That is the case for an independent survey in a sentence. Somebody needs to work out which problem you have before anybody sells you the fix for one of them.

What a DampScan survey in Ramsgate covers

A survey here is a morning or an afternoon with one qualified surveyor, not a salesman with a meter. In Ramsgate that means:

  • Moisture readings across a grid on every affected wall, not just the stain, so the shape of the damp is on record
  • A look at Regency stucco, brick and flint or brick outside, joint by joint where it matters, because the cause is usually on the outside face
  • Ground and path levels against each elevation, measured against the damp proof course or the internal floor
  • Gutters, downpipes, gullies and any drain covers we can lift
  • Air temperature, humidity and surface temperatures, so condensation is measured rather than guessed
  • Ventilation: whether fans extract to outside and how much air they actually move
  • Floor voids and exposed timbers where they can be reached, for rot and beetle

A sample report, in outline

No two reports are the same, but a Ramsgate report usually runs in this order:

  • Property and conditions on the day, including recent weather
  • Findings by room and by elevation, each with photographs
  • Readings and the pattern they make, not a single headline figure
  • Diagnosis: the cause, and the alternatives that were tested and excluded
  • Recommendations as a specification, with the order to do them in
  • Anything not inspected, and exactly where to look if it needs opening up

Buying or letting in Ramsgate

Ramsgate has attracted many buyers to its Regency terraces and harbourside flats. Basements, stucco and parapets can hide significant work, and responsibility in converted buildings is often shared. An independent survey before exchange sets out what is wrong, what it will take and who should pay.

Letting in Ramsgate? Damp and mould in rented homes are a hazard under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System, and social landlords in England now have fixed timescales to investigate and act under Awaab's Law, with the government planning to extend similar duties to private renting. Damp survey questions from Ramsgate

Our lower ground floor is damp at the back. Is that rising damp?

Usually it is lateral water from the slope behind, which the rear wall retains. That needs drainage and, where the room is habitable, a below ground system, not injection.

Can stucco be repaired rather than replaced?

Often yes, in compatible materials, followed by a breathable paint. Replacing it with cement render and sealing it with plastic paint tends to make old walls wetter.

Our flint wall has loose flints and damp patches. Why?

Commonly because it was repointed in cement, which trapped moisture and let frost loosen the flints. Lime repointing deals with both.

Do you cover Minster, Manston and Cliffsend?

Yes, and Broadstairs, Margate and the rest of Thanet.

Is woodworm in a Regency house always active?

No. Much of it is historic. We check timber moisture and look for fresh signs before saying whether treatment is needed.
