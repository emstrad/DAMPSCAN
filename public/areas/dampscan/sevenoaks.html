Damp survey in Sevenoaks

Sevenoaks sits on the greensand ridge with Knole on one side and the Darent valley on the other, and its housing ranges from medieval and Georgian buildings on the High Street through large Victorian and Edwardian houses to interwar and post war roads around the station and Riverhead. Around it lie Wealden villages with weatherboarded cottages, tile hung elevations, timber frames and converted oasts. These are the buildings where a modern damp treatment is most likely to do harm, and where the right answer is usually maintenance rather than intervention.

Sevenoaks homes, and how they get damp

Weatherboarding is a rain screen. It keeps water off the structure behind while allowing air to move, and it depends entirely on being maintained. Boards that have cupped, split at the fixings, or been repainted repeatedly until the laps are sealed will hold water rather than shed it. Where the boarding meets the ground, or a later path or patio has come up to it, the bottom boards and the sole plate behind them are the first casualties, and that sole plate is structural.

Converted oast houses bring a specific set of problems. A roundel was built as an agricultural drying kiln, with a floor designed to let air through and a cowl at the top to draw it. Converting one into habitable rooms means closing all of that up. The wall is often single skin brick, sometimes with no damp proof course at all, and the new insulation and plaster sit directly against it. Damp in a converted oast is nearly always about how the conversion handled ventilation and the wall build up.

In the town itself, the large Victorian and Edwardian houses on Kippington and around the Vine are solid walled, often with ragstone or tile hung elevations, cellars and big roofs with many valleys and parapets. Their damp is mostly rainwater: valleys, hoppers and flashings that have been overlooked for years. The timber framed cottages in the villages need lime, not cement, and finishes that let the frame dry, and gardens around them have often risen until they sit above the internal floor.

Ground, water and weather in Sevenoaks

Sevenoaks stands on the Lower Greensand, the Hythe Beds of sandy limestone and ragstone that form the ridge, with Gault clay in the vale below at Otford and Kemsing and the chalk of the North Downs beyond. The greensand drains freely, so on the ridge damp is usually rainwater rather than ground water. Springs break out where the sands meet the clay, and houses along that line, and in the Darent valley, can sit in much wetter ground.

What we find most often in Sevenoaks

Three kinds of damp, three different fixes

Rising damp needs the ground moisture stopped at the base of the wall. Penetrating damp needs the route in closed: a gutter, a joint, a sill, a flashing. Condensation needs less moisture in the air, more air movement and warmer surfaces. In Sevenoaks, as everywhere, these get confused constantly, and a remedy for one does nothing for the others.

That is the case for an independent survey in a sentence. Somebody needs to work out which problem you have before anybody sells you the fix for one of them.

What we check on a damp survey in Sevenoaks

The visit follows the water. Whatever brought you to us, in Sevenoaks we work through:

  • The roofline and chimney from the ground and from windows, then gutters and downpipes in detail
  • Every outside wall for cracks, open joints, failed render and anything bridging the base of the wall
  • Airbricks and sub floor ventilation, often buried by later paths and extensions
  • Internal walls with calibrated readings at height bands, so rising, penetrating and condensation damp can be told apart
  • Surface and air conditions in the problem rooms, logged rather than estimated
  • Kitchen and bathroom extraction, measured at the grille
  • Any earlier damp proofing, its paperwork and whether it was ever the right answer

Buying or letting in Sevenoaks

Buying in Sevenoaks often means a substantial period house or a cottage in one of the villages, and a building survey that recommends specialist investigation of damp and timber. Commissioning that from a firm that sells treatment invites a long quote. An independent survey tells you what is active, what is historic and what is simply maintenance, so the negotiation is about real costs.

Letting in Sevenoaks? Damp and mould in rented homes are a hazard under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System, and social landlords in England now have fixed timescales to investigate and act under Awaab's Law, with the government planning to extend similar duties to private renting. Damp survey questions from Sevenoaks

We have woodworm. Does the whole house need treating?

Usually not, and blanket treatment is one of the more common overspecifications we are asked to review. The first question is whether the infestation is active or historic, which can be established from the flight holes, the presence of frass and the moisture content of the timber. Most beetle activity depends on damp timber, so the durable answer is normally to fix why the timber is wet.

Is a converted oast house harder to survey?

It is different rather than harder, and it needs somebody who knows what the building was originally for. What matters is how the wall build up handles moisture, what happened to the original ventilation at the floor and the cowl, and whether a damp proof course exists at all. Those are the questions the survey is aimed at.

Our big Victorian house has damp on the top floor. Where is it coming from?

Almost always the roof drainage: a valley gutter, a parapet, a hopper or a flashing. Large period roofs have many of these and they are rarely inspected. The survey traces the water and identifies the point of entry so a roofer can repair it.

Do you cover Westerham, Borough Green and the villages?

Yes, and Otford, Kemsing, Seal, Ightham, Shoreham and the rest of the district, as well as Swanley and Edenbridge.

Our cellar in Sevenoaks is damp. Is that a problem?

Cellars were built to be cool and somewhat damp. Whether that is a problem depends on how you want to use it. If it is to become a room, it needs a system designed for the water present, chosen after the survey.
