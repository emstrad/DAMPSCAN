Damp survey in Aylesford

Aylesford village gathers around a medieval bridge over the Medway, with a ragstone church above and old houses running down to the river. Around it the parish takes in Eccles, a village built for the cement works, Blue Bell Hill up on the Downs, and large twentieth century and newer estates towards Ditton and Larkfield. The river is the defining fact: it brings flood risk to the low ground and wet air to the old buildings along it.

Aylesford homes, and how they get damp

The old village has ragstone and brick houses and cottages, some timber framed, many listed and in a conservation area that runs along the river. Walls closest to the water sit in ground that is wet much of the year, and the lower parts of the village have flooded in the past. In these buildings, damp at low level is often groundwater and river influence, not a failed damp proof course, and a survey needs to separate seasonal river related damp from defects that can be fixed.

Eccles and Burham, across the river, grew around cement and lime works, and still have rows of Victorian and early twentieth century terraced cottages. Solid brick walls, small rear yards and limited ventilation are typical, along with chimneys that have been capped without vents and gardens that have risen over a century.

Much of the housing between Aylesford and Ditton, and around Preston Hall and Royal British Legion Village, is twentieth century and newer, with cavity walls and modern damp proof courses. The damp there is mainly condensation, bridged courses and ventilation, and it is measured rather than guessed at.

Ground, water and weather in Aylesford

Aylesford lies where the Medway cuts through towards the North Downs gap. The valley floor is river gravels and alluvium, the slopes rise through sands and clays to the chalk of Blue Bell Hill. Sand and gravel extraction and the old paper and cement industries have reshaped much of the low ground, and lakes now sit where pits were dug. The water table across the valley floor is high, and it rises with the river after prolonged rain.

Rising damp, penetrating damp or condensation?

They can look the same on a Aylesford wall: a stain, a tide mark, blown plaster or mould. They behave differently. Rising damp is steady through the year and highest at the base of a wall. Penetrating damp follows the weather and sits where water has a route in, around a window, under a gutter, on the exposed side. Condensation is worst in cold weather, on the coldest surfaces, and often comes with streaming windows.

A meter alone cannot tell them apart, and salts in old plaster make every damp meter read high. The pattern, the readings at depth, the outside of the building and the room conditions together can, which is why the survey takes the time it does.

Inside and out: the Aylesford survey, step by step

Most damp is decided outside the house, so that is where half the visit goes. On a typical Aylesford property we check:

  • The outside walls first: pointing, render, sills, and how Kentish ragstone and brick has been repaired over the years
  • Where the ground sits against the wall, front and back, and whether a patio or flower bed has crept up
  • Rainwater: gutters, hoppers, downpipes and where they discharge, ideally after we have seen them in rain
  • Damp readings inside on a pattern, with a reference reading from a dry wall for comparison
  • Salts, tide marks and blown plaster, which say what the water has been doing for years
  • Room conditions: humidity, dew point and the coldest surfaces, for any mould
  • Timber in reach: skirtings, joist ends, sub floor and the loft hatch view of the roof

A sample report, in outline

No two reports are the same, but a Aylesford report usually runs in this order:

  • Property and conditions on the day, including recent weather
  • Findings by room and by elevation, each with photographs
  • Readings and the pattern they make, not a single headline figure
  • Diagnosis: the cause, and the alternatives that were tested and excluded
  • Recommendations as a specification, with the order to do them in
  • Anything not inspected, and exactly where to look if it needs opening up

Buying or letting in Aylesford

If you are buying close to the river in Aylesford, it is worth knowing how much of any damp is seasonal and river related and how much is a defect, because the first is about management and flood resilience and the second can be fixed. An independent survey before exchange separates them, and the report is written in a way that is useful when you speak to insurers and to the seller.

Letting in Aylesford? Damp and mould in rented homes are a hazard under the Housing Health and Safety Rating System, and social landlords in England now have fixed timescales to investigate and act under Awaab's Law, with the government planning to extend similar duties to private renting. An independent report that separates building defects from condensation is the evidence both landlords and tenants need. See our guide to landlord damp and mould duties.

Damp survey questions from Aylesford

We live near the river in Aylesford and the floor is damp. Can damp proofing stop it?

Not in the usual sense. Damp from a high water table or river influence needs a different approach: drainage, floor construction and flood resilient finishes. A chemical damp proof course is designed for moisture rising up a wall, not for groundwater under a floor.

Is our listed cottage at risk from cement repairs?

If it has been repointed or rendered in cement, yes, especially close to the river where the walls take up more moisture. Cement stops a stone or soft brick wall drying. The survey checks for it and the report recommends lime where it is needed.

Our Eccles terrace has damp on the chimney breast. What causes that?

Often a chimney that has been capped without ventilation, so moisture condenses inside the flue and soaks through, or a defective flashing at the stack. Both are fixed at the chimney, not by damp proofing the breast.

Do you cover Ditton, Larkfield and Blue Bell Hill?

Yes, along with Burham, Wouldham, Eccles and the rest of the parish and the Malling area.

We have a new house near Preston Hall with mould. Is it a defect?

It can be. Mould in a modern house is usually ventilation or a construction detail. We measure ventilation and humidity and record the cause so it can be raised with the developer if it is a defect.

Will you try to sell me damp proofing in Aylesford?

No. DampScan is a survey and report practice and carries out no remedial work of any kind. Where work is needed, the report specifies it so any contractor can price it, and where it is not, the report says so.
