Maintenance plans

Nearly every emergency we attend was preventable and visible a year earlier. An annual check costs a fraction of the repair it avoids.

What a visit covers

Gutters and valleys cleared and flushed, and the falls checked with water. Coverings looked over for slipped, cracked or delaminating units. Flashings, ridge and verge checked for movement. Moss dealt with where it is holding water against the covering, and left alone where it is doing no harm, which is more often than the pressure-washing trade would have you believe.

Small things get fixed on the visit: a slipped tile re-hung, a loose bracket refixed, a short length of pointing made good. Anything larger is photographed and priced separately rather than started without asking.

The photo report is the point

Each visit produces a short report with dated photographs. Over a few years that becomes a record of how the roof is actually ageing, which is what turns a guess into a decision when somebody asks whether it needs replacing.

For managing agents and landlords it is also the document that settles an argument with a leaseholder or an insurer, because it shows the condition before the incident rather than after it.

Who it is worth it for

Anyone with a commercial roof, a managed block, or a let portfolio, where an unplanned failure costs occupancy as well as repair. Anyone with a lot of trees near the house. Anyone with a roof they cannot see from anywhere on the ground, which is most terraced streets.

It is not worth it on a five year old roof in an open position with no trees. We will say so.

Questions

How often?

Annually for most buildings, six-monthly where there are trees, valleys or box gutters involved.

Is it a contract?

It is an arrangement, not a lock-in. If a year goes by and you want to stop, stop.
