Storm damage and insurance work

After high winds the priority is making the building safe and watertight. The second priority, and the one people forget in the moment, is documenting it properly.

What we do first

Temporary make-safe. Removing what is loose before it comes down on somebody, sheeting where the covering has gone, and clearing what has already fallen. On a stack that has shifted or a section of roof that has lifted, that sometimes means taking more off before anything goes back on.

We photograph everything before we touch it, because the evidence stops existing the moment we make it safe.

What an insurer actually needs

A dated photographic record of the damage, a plain statement of cause, and a quotation scoped line by line rather than a one-line invoice. Claims stall on vagueness far more often than on the money: an adjuster cannot approve "repair roof, £4,000".

We write quotes in that format as standard, so the same document works for you and for them. Where an adjuster wants something specific, tell us and we will provide it rather than sending you back and forth.

What is storm damage and what is not

Insurers distinguish storm damage from wear. A tile lifted by a gale is storm damage; twenty years of nail fatigue that finally let go in a breeze is maintenance, and an adjuster will say so. We tell you honestly which one we think you have before you make a claim, because a declined claim is worse than no claim.

Where it is genuinely a mixture, which it often is, we quote the storm element separately so the claim covers what it should and you decide about the rest.

Do not let the emergency decide the permanent repair

The pressure after a storm is to have somebody, anybody, on the roof today. That is how people end up with a make-safe billed as a repair. Make safe now, then take the time to look at the permanent work properly, with photographs and a fixed price, in daylight.

Questions

Can you come out at short notice?

Emergencies are prioritised. Tell us at the enquiry if water is coming in.

Do you deal with the insurer for me?

We provide the evidence and the scoped quotation in the format they expect, and answer questions they put to us. The claim stays yours.
